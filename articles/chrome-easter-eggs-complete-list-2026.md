## The Browser That Runs on Dino

Perhaps the most famous Chrome easter egg is the dinosaur game that appears when you are offline. If you search for chrome easter eggs complete list 2026, this one tops almost every list because it is the most widely known hidden feature in the browser.

To access this easter egg, simply disconnect your internet connection and try loading any webpage. Instead of the usual error page, you will see a pixelated dinosaur with the message "No internet" below it. Press the spacebar or up arrow key to make the dinosaur jump over cacti. The longer you play, the faster the game becomes. This endless runner game has become so popular that you can now play it even when you have an internet connection by typing "chrome://dino" in your address bar.

## The Gravity Flip Trick

To return to normal, simply click anywhere on the page or refresh. ## Hidden Games in Chrome Flags

Chrome includes experimental features that users can enable through the hidden flags page. Some of these flags contain hidden games and interactive elements that are not immediately obvious.

To access these features, type "chrome://flags" in your address bar and press Enter. You will see a page full of experimental settings. Look for options related to "Game" or "Easter Egg" in the search box within that page. Some versions of Chrome have included hidden games within these experimental features, though availability varies by version.

Be careful when changing flags, as experimental features can sometimes cause unexpected behavior in your browser. Only change settings you understand, and consider resetting flags to default after exploring.

## The Omnibox Calculator

Chrome's address bar, called the omnibox, is more powerful than most users realize. It can perform calculations directly without you needing to open a calculator app. This is not exactly an easter egg, but it is a hidden capability that surprises many people.

Simply type a mathematical expression like "15 * 27 + 42" or "sqrt(144)" in the omnibox and press Enter. Chrome will display the answer at the top of the search results. You can also convert units by typing something like "10 miles in kilometers" or "100 fahrenheit in celsius". This feature works because Chrome integrates with Google's search capabilities to provide instant answers.

## Secret URLs to Explore

Chrome contains numerous internal pages that reveal information about your browser and sometimes include hidden features. Typing "chrome://version" shows detailed information about your Chrome installation. The page "chrome://chrome-urls" provides a list of all internal Chrome pages, some of which contain interesting features worth exploring.

Another useful internal page is "chrome://history", which opens your browsing history directly. While not an easter egg, it is a helpful shortcut that many users do not know exists. Similarly, "chrome://settings" takes you directly to Chrome's settings page without needing to navigate through menus.
