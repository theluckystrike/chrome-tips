# Best Chrome Extensions for Designers 2026

If you are a designer in 2026, your Chrome browser is probably one of your most important work tools. Whether you are researching inspiration, inspecting websites, gathering color palettes, or collaborating with clients, the right extensions can dramatically speed up your workflow and make your creative process much more enjoyable. This guide covers the best Chrome extensions for designers that you should consider adding to your browser this year.

Design work often involves juggling multiple browser tabs, constantly switching between inspiration sources and design tools, and performing repetitive tasks like color picking or font identification. The extensions in this guide address these common challenges and more. Some help you work more efficiently, while others provide features that would otherwise require expensive desktop software. All of them are free or offer generous free tiers, so you can try them without any financial commitment.

## ColorZilla: The Ultimate Color Picker for Designers

ColorZilla has been a staple in the design community for years, and it remains one of the most essential extensions for any designer working in Chrome. This powerful tool allows you to pick colors from any webpage with a single click, eliminating the need to manually recreate colors or take screenshots to extract color values.

When you install ColorZilla, you gain access to an advanced color picker that can sample any color visible on your screen. Simply click the extension icon or use the keyboard shortcut, and your cursor becomes a color sampler. Click on any element, and ColorZilla instantly provides you with the exact color value in multiple formats including HEX, RGB, HSL, and more.

What makes ColorZilla particularly valuable for designers is its color history feature. As you pick colors throughout your workday, they are automatically saved to a history list that you can access later. This is incredibly useful when you are working on a project and need to reference colors you discovered earlier but did not write down. You can build a personal library of brand colors, inspiration shades, and palette components without any additional effort.

The extension also includes a gradient generator that helps you create CSS gradients visually. You can add multiple color stops, adjust their positions, and preview the result in real-time. When you are happy with your gradient, you can copy the CSS code directly to your clipboard. This feature alone saves designers significant time when implementing gradient backgrounds or buttons in their designs.

For designers who work with color palettes regularly, ColorZilla offers a palette viewer that shows all the colors used on the current webpage. This is particularly useful when you encounter a website with a beautiful color scheme and want to understand its color makeup. You can quickly see all the colors used, their approximate proportions, and export them for your own projects.

## WhatFont: Instantly Identify Any Font You See

Font identification has always been a challenge for designers. When you see a beautiful typeface on a website, figuring out its name and details used to require screenshots, manual research, or guesswork. WhatFont simplifies this process dramatically by letting you identify fonts with a simple hover.

The extension works by adding a toolbar button that, when clicked, turns your cursor into a font identifier. Hover over any text on any webpage, and WhatFont displays a popup showing the font name, font family, size, weight, color, and line height. This information appears instantly, allowing you to research and source fonts much faster than traditional methods.

WhatFont supports all major web font services including Google Fonts, Adobe Fonts, Typekit, and many others. When you hover over text that uses a web font from these services, the extension provides a direct link to the font page where you can learn more or download it. This makes font research an incredibly streamlined process.

For designers who frequently need to document font choices or share them with team members, WhatFont includes a copy feature that lets you quickly copy font details to your clipboard. You can paste this information into design documents, style guides, or communication with clients. The extension also saves your font history, so you can review fonts you have identified in previous sessions.

The extension has evolved over the years to include additional features that designers appreciate. You can now preview how text would look in different fonts without actually changing the website. This is useful for quick typography experiments or when you want to test readability in various typefaces. WhatFont has truly become an indispensable tool for any designer who works with web typography.

## VisBug: Interactive Design Inspector

VisBug represents a new generation of design tools that make web inspection accessible to designers without requiring technical knowledge. While Chrome's built-in developer tools are powerful, they can be overwhelming for designers who are not developers. VisBug simplifies the process by providing intuitive visual controls for exploring and understanding web design.

When you activate VisBug, you gain access to a suite of inspection tools that let you measure distances, identify colors, check alignment, and examine spacing. The extension overlays helpful guides and measurements directly on the webpage, making it easy to understand how a design is constructed. You can see exactly how much padding surrounds an element, what margins separate different sections, and how elements are aligned relative to each other.

One of VisBug's most powerful features is its accessibility checker. The extension can analyze any webpage and highlight elements that may have accessibility issues, such as insufficient color contrast, missing alt text, or improper heading hierarchy. This helps designers ensure that their work meets accessibility standards without needing separate testing tools or expertise.

The extension also includes design annotation tools that are perfect for collaboration. You can leave comments on specific elements, highlight issues, or suggest improvements. When working with clients or developers, these annotations provide clear, visual feedback that is much more effective than written descriptions. You can export your annotations as images to share in presentations or documentation.

VisBug's color tools complement other extensions nicely. You can pick colors, view the color palette of a page, and check contrast ratios between any two colors. The contrast checker is particularly valuable for ensuring text readability and meeting WCAG accessibility guidelines. Having these capabilities integrated into a single, designer-friendly interface makes VisBug a great addition to any designer's toolkit.

## Muzli: Your Daily Design Inspiration Stream

Staying inspired is crucial for designers, and Muzli helps by curating a feed of beautiful design content from across the internet. Rather than spending time searching for inspiration manually, Muzli delivers a constant stream of carefully selected design examples directly to your browser.

When you install Muzli, it replaces your new tab page with a visually stunning feed of design inspiration. This includes website designs, mobile app interfaces, typography examples, illustrations, and more. The content is sourced from popular design platforms like Dribbble, Behance, and various design blogs, ensuring you see high-quality work.

What makes Muzli particularly valuable is its personalization features. You can select the design categories that interest you, whether you focus on web design, mobile apps, branding, or other areas. The algorithm then tailors your feed to show content that matches your interests. You can also save inspiration to collections for later reference, making it easy to build mood boards for specific projects.

For designers who work in specific industries, Muzli offers curated feeds for different design fields. Whether you are interested in e-commerce design, fintech interfaces, or entertainment apps, you can find feeds that showcase the best work in your area of focus. This helps you stay aware of current design trends and best practices in your specific domain.

Muzli also includes features that help you share inspiration with others. You can send designs directly to team members or clients, making it easy to communicate about visual direction. The extension integrates with popular design tools and platforms, streamlining your workflow when inspiration leads to actual design work. For designers who want to stay inspired without hunting for content manually, Muzli is an excellent choice.

## CSS Peeper: See the Style Behind the Sites

CSS Peeper gives designers X-ray vision into the CSS styling of any webpage. While tools like ColorZilla and WhatFont focus on specific properties, CSS Peeper provides a comprehensive view of all the styling applied to any element. This makes it invaluable for designers who want to understand how professional websites are built or learn from specific design implementations.

The extension works by adding an eye icon to your toolbar. When you click it and then hover over any element on a page, CSS Peeper displays a panel with all the relevant CSS properties. This includes typography settings like font family, size, weight, and line height. It shows spacing information including padding, margins, and gaps. It reveals color values, border properties, shadows, and background styles. Essentially, anything that can be styled with CSS becomes visible.

What sets CSS Peeper apart from Chrome's built-in inspector is its design-focused interface. The information is presented in a clean, organized way that makes sense to designers. Property names and values are easy to read, and the panel is visually less intimidating than developer tools. You can quickly scan through styling information without getting bogged down in technical details.

The extension also includes a page overview feature that shows you all the colors, fonts, and images used on a webpage. This gives you a quick summary of a site's visual identity, which is useful for research or competitive analysis. You can see at a glance what color palette a website uses, what fonts are primary and secondary, and what image styles are employed.

For designers who want to implement specific styles they discover on the web, CSS Peeper provides export functionality. You can copy individual property values or export entire style blocks. This makes it easy to take inspiration and apply it to your own projects. The extension bridges the gap between seeing something you like and actually implementing it in your work.

## Managing Your Design Workflow

While the extensions above focus on specific design tasks, keeping your browser running smoothly is equally important for productivity. Designers often have many tabs open simultaneously, with research, inspiration, client sites, and design tools all competing for attention. This can strain your computer's resources and slow down your workflow.

Tab Suspender Pro helps manage this by automatically suspending tabs that you have not used recently. When you have dozens of tabs open researching different aspects of a project, the ones you are not actively viewing still consume memory and processing power. Tab Suspender Pro detects which tabs are idle and temporarily pauses them, freeing up resources for the work you are doing.

The great thing about Tab Suspender Pro from a designer's perspective is that suspended tabs are not lost. They remain in your tab bar and can be restored instantly with a single click. When you need to reference something you were looking at earlier, the tab wakes up immediately. This means you can keep all your research and inspiration available without sacrificing performance.

For designers who work on multiple projects simultaneously, Tab Suspender Pro offers project-based organization. You can group tabs by project and suspend entire groups when you are not working on them. This keeps your workspace organized and ensures that your browser only uses resources for the project you are actively developing. The extension has become essential for many designers who need to manage complex, multi-project workflows.

## Additional Extensions Worth Considering

Beyond the main extensions covered above, several other tools can enhance a designer's Chrome experience. These complement the core extensions and address specific needs that many designers face in their daily work.

For screenshot and annotation, tools like Lightshot or Nimbus Screenshot let you capture specific areas of your screen, annotate them with arrows, text, or shapes, and share immediately. This is invaluable for client communication, as you can visually point to exactly what you mean rather than describing it in words.

If you need to collaborate with developers or handoff designs, the various design handoff extensions help bridge the gap between visual design and implementation. These tools generate style guides, export assets, and provide implementation specifications that make the developer handoff process smoother.

For designers who work with prototypes or interactive designs, extensions that integrate with tools like Figma or Adobe XD can streamline your workflow. You can preview designs in context, access design files quickly, and share work without leaving your browser.

Browser history management extensions can help you find websites you visited but did not bookmark. As a designer who researches constantly, this feature alone can save hours of time over weeks and months of work.

## Building Your Perfect Extension Toolkit

The best extension setup varies depending on your specific work, but the extensions covered in this guide address the most common needs for designers. Start with ColorZilla and WhatFont, as these solve everyday problems that occur in almost every design project. Add VisBug if you need accessible inspection tools, Muzli for inspiration management, and CSS Peeper for understanding how other websites are styled.

As you work, pay attention to what tasks feel repetitive or time-consuming. These are often the areas where additional extensions can help. The Chrome Web Store has a vast selection of extensions, and new tools are constantly emerging. Taking time occasionally to explore new options can reveal tools that significantly improve your workflow.

Remember that extensions do consume some browser resources, so it is worth periodically reviewing what you have installed. Remove extensions you no longer use, and ensure that your active extensions are from trusted developers. Keeping your extension list lean helps maintain browser performance while ensuring you have exactly the tools you need.

The right extensions transform Chrome from a basic web browser into a powerful design workstation. By investing a little time in setting up your toolkit, you can work more efficiently, stay inspired, and produce better design work. The extensions in this guide provide an excellent starting point for building your perfect design extension collection.
