# Chrome for Microsoft Teams in Browser Tips

If you are looking for chrome for microsoft teams in browser tips, you have come to the right place. Using Microsoft Teams through Chrome is a convenient way to stay connected with your team without installing the desktop app. However, it can sometimes feel slow or use too much memory, especially when you have many tabs open. This guide shares practical tips to make Teams run smoother in your browser.

## Why Teams Can Feel Slow in Chrome

Microsoft Teams runs as a web application inside your browser. It needs to constantly check for new messages, update notifications, keep video calls active, and sync files in the background. All of these activities require memory and processing power from your computer.

When you have Teams open along with your email, calendar, documents, and other web apps, Chrome has to divide its resources among everything. This is why Teams might feel perfectly fine when you first open it but gradually becomes sluggish as your workday progresses and you open more tabs.

Understanding this helps you see why certain tips work. The goal is to reduce the overall load on Chrome so Teams has more resources available to run smoothly.

## Keep Only Necessary Tabs Open

One of the simplest ways to improve Teams performance is to close tabs you are not using. Every open tab consumes memory, even if you are not looking at it. When you have twenty or thirty tabs open, Chrome has less capacity to keep Teams running smoothly.

Get into the habit of closing tabs when you are done with them. If you need to save a page for later, use bookmarks instead of leaving the tab open. This small change can make a noticeable difference in how responsive Teams feels throughout the day.

## Use Chrome Memory Saver Mode

Chrome has a built-in feature called Memory Saver that helps your browser use less memory. When enabled, it automatically puts tabs you have not used recently into a sleep state. This frees up memory for the tabs you are actively using, like Teams.

To turn on Memory Saver, open Chrome and click the three dots in the top right corner. Go to Settings, then look for the Performance section. Toggle Memory Saver on. You can also choose which sites should always stay active, such as Teams. Adding Teams to this list ensures it stays fully loaded and responsive while other tabs take a nap.

This feature is particularly helpful if you tend to keep many tabs open throughout your workday. It works automatically in the background, so you do not have to think about it once you turn it on.

## Consider Using a Tab Management Extension

If you find yourself struggling with too many open tabs, a tab management extension can help. These tools let you organize tabs into groups, save sets of tabs for later, and quickly find what you need without cluttering your browser window.

There are several extensions available that work well with Teams. Some allow you to create separate workspaces for different projects, while others let you quickly save and restore tab sessions. Finding one that fits your workflow can help you keep your browser organized and your Teams experience smooth.

## Give Teams Its Own Window

Instead of keeping Teams as one tab among many, try giving it its own dedicated browser window. This can help Chrome allocate resources more efficiently and make it easier to find Teams when you need it.

To do this, open Teams in a new window by clicking the three dots in the Teams interface and selecting Open in New Window. Pin this window so it stays easily accessible. Having Teams in its own window also makes it easier to switch between your work and other tasks without losing your place in a conversation.

## Manage Notifications Wisely

Teams can generate many notifications throughout the day, and each one requires Chrome to do a small amount of work. While you probably want to stay informed, managing which notifications you receive can help reduce the background load.

In Teams settings, review your notification preferences. Consider turning off notifications for channels or conversations that are less important. You can also adjust how Chrome handles notifications by going to Settings, then Privacy and Security, and finally Site Settings. Reducing unnecessary notifications can help Teams run more efficiently.

## Keep Chrome Updated

Chrome regularly releases updates that include performance improvements and bug fixes. Using an outdated version of Chrome can sometimes cause Teams to run slower or behave unexpectedly.

To check if Chrome is up to date, click the three dots in the top right corner and look for an update option in the menu. If an update is available, install it and restart your browser. Keeping Chrome updated ensures you get the best possible experience with Teams and other web applications.

## Clear Browser Cache Regularly

Over time, Chrome stores cached data from websites to load them faster in the future. However, this cache can grow large and sometimes cause issues with web applications like Teams. Clearing your cache periodically can help maintain good performance.

To clear cache in Chrome, click the three dots, go to Settings, then Privacy and Security, and select Clear Browsing Data. Choose a time range and make sureCached images and files is selected. You do not need to clear this very often, but doing so every few weeks can help keep things running smoothly.

## Use Tab Suspender Pro for Extra Help

If you want additional control over how Chrome handles your tabs, consider trying Tab Suspender Pro. This extension gives you more options for managing inactive tabs beyond what Chrome built-in Memory Saver offers. You can customize which tabs suspend, how quickly they suspend, and what happens when you return to them.

Tab Suspender Pro lets you set different rules for different types of tabs. For example, you might want Teams to never suspend while allowing other less important tabs to sleep quickly. This level of control can help you balance keeping Teams responsive while still managing your overall browser memory usage.

## Check Your Internet Connection

Sometimes slowness in Teams is not caused by Chrome at all but by your internet connection. If Teams feels laggy, try checking your connection speed and stability. You can use online speed test tools to see how your connection is performing.

If you notice frequent drops or slow speeds, try moving closer to your router or connecting via ethernet cable instead of WiFi. A stable connection is just as important for good Teams performance as having enough memory in your browser.

## Restart Chrome Periodically

Like any software, Chrome can benefit from occasional restarts. Over time, memory can become fragmented, and background processes can accumulate. Closing Chrome completely and opening it again fresh can help restore performance.

Try restarting Chrome at the start of your workday or after lunch. This simple habit can help keep Teams and your other web apps running smoothly throughout the day.
