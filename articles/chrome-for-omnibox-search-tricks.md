# Chrome for Omnibox Search Tricks

If you have ever searched for chrome for omnibox search tricks, you probably wanted to get more out of your browser's address bar. The truth is that Chrome's omnibox is one of the most powerful tools hiding in plain sight on your computer. Most people only use it to type website addresses, but it can do so much more. With a few simple tricks, you can turn that address bar into a calculator, converter, dictionary, and search powerhouse that will save you time every day.

## Turn Your Address Bar Into a Calculator

One of the most useful omnibox tricks is using Chrome as an instant calculator. You do not need to open a calculator app or search for one online. Just type your math problem directly into the address bar and press Enter.

You can do simple arithmetic like "125 + 47" or "200 - 55." You can also handle multiplication with "15 * 12" or division with "300 / 8." But it gets even better. Chrome can handle percentages too. Try typing "20% of 150" to quickly calculate a tip or discount. You can even combine operations like "100 + (25% of 100)" for more complex math. This comes in handy when you are shopping and need to figure out prices quickly without pulling out your phone.

## Convert Currencies and Units While You Browse

Another fantastic feature built into Chrome is automatic conversion. Whether you are shopping internationally or working with measurements, you can convert right from the omnibox without visiting a conversion website.

For currency conversions, type something like "50 USD to euros" or "100 dollars in pounds." Chrome will show you the current exchange rate and converted amount at the top of your search results. You can convert between almost any major currency in the world.

For unit conversions, the possibilities are endless. Type "30 miles in kilometers" to see distance conversions, "150 pounds in kilograms" for weight, or "350 fahrenheit in celsius" for temperature. You can also convert time zones, data sizes, and even cooking measurements. This is incredibly useful for anyone who works across international systems or travels frequently.

## Get Quick Definitions Without Leaving Your Page

Have you ever been reading an article and stumbled upon a word you did not know? Instead of opening a new tab to look it up, you can get a definition instantly from Chrome. Just type "define" followed by the word.

For example, type "define serendipity" or "define algorithm" and Chrome will display the definition at the top of your search results. This works for technical terms, slang, foreign words, and just about any word you can think of. It keeps you in your flow while browsing and makes learning new vocabulary effortless.

## Search Specific Websites Directly

Sometimes you need to find something on a particular website, but that site might not have a great search feature. Chrome lets you search any website directly from the omnibox using the "site:" operator.

Type "recipes site:bbcgoodfood.com" to find recipes only on BBC Good Food. Type "reviews site:techcrunch.com" to find articles on TechCrunch. This is incredibly powerful for finding specific content on large websites or when you remember reading something somewhere but cannot remember the exact page. You can combine this with any search term to narrow down results to exactly the site you want.

## Find Images Faster

If you need images for a project or just want to browse pictures, you can search images directly from the omnibox. Just type "images of" followed by what you are looking for.

For example, type "images of modern kitchen designs" or "images of cute puppies" and Chrome will show you image results immediately. You do not need to visit Google Images separately. This saves you a click and gets you to visual results faster.

## Track Packages and Flights Instantly

When you have a tracking number for a package or a flight number, Chrome can show you the status right from the address bar. Simply type your tracking number or flight number and press Enter.

Chrome works with major shipping carriers to show you where your package is and when it might arrive. For flights, you can see departure times, arrival times, and any delays. This is much faster than visiting a carrier website and entering tracking information manually.

## Search Your Chrome History Faster

If you have visited a page before and want to go back, you can search your history directly from the omnibox. Just type part of the page title or URL and Chrome will show you matching results from your browsing history.

This is faster than clicking through your history or trying to remember exactly when you visited a site. It works especially well for pages you visit frequently or that have distinctive names. You can also use this to quickly reopen tabs you accidentally closed.

## Use Voice Search When Your Hands Are Busy

If your hands are full or you are multitasking, you can use voice search through the omnibox. Click the microphone icon in the search box or, if enabled, say "Hey Google" followed by your search. This works on desktop when you have a microphone connected.

Voice search is great for hands-free browsing, quick questions, or when you need to search while doing something else. It is one of those features that feels like magic once you start using it regularly.

## Quick Links for Even Faster Access

Chrome allows you to set up custom search engines for even faster access to your favorite sites. While this requires a tiny bit of setup, it pays off if you use certain websites frequently.

For example, you can set up "g" to search Google, "yt" to search YouTube, or "wiki" to search Wikipedia. Once configured, you just type the shortcut and your search term, and Chrome will take you directly to the results. This eliminates extra clicks and makes your browsing workflow much smoother.
