Chrome how to block specific website notifications is something many Chrome users need to figure out when certain sites become too pushy with their alerts. Maybe a news site you visited once keeps sending you breaking news alerts, or an online store won't stop telling you about sales. The good news is that Chrome lets you block notifications from individual sites while still allowing ones you actually want.

Let me walk you through the simple process of taking back control over which websites can send you notifications.

## Why You Might Want to Block Certain Site Notifications

Websites ask for notification permission when you first visit them, and many people click Allow without thinking much about it. Over time, you might find that several sites have permission to send you alerts, and not all of them are helpful. Some sites send too many notifications, while others you may have only visited once and never intended to hear from again.

The key thing to understand is that you do not have to choose between allowing all notifications or blocking them all. Chrome gives you granular control to handle each website individually.

## Finding Your Notification Settings

The first step is to open Chrome settings where all your notification permissions are stored. Click the three dots in the upper right corner of your Chrome window to open the menu, then select Settings. On the settings page, look for Privacy and security in the left sidebar and click on it. From there, click Site settings, then scroll down until you see Permissions. Click on Notifications.

You will now see a list of websites that are allowed to send you notifications. This is your control center for managing which sites can reach you.

## Blocking a Specific Website

To block notifications from a specific website, find it in the list of allowed sites. Each website entry has a three dots menu next to it. Click on those three dots, and you will see options to Remove or Block that website. Click Block, and the site will no longer be able to send you notifications.

If you do not see the website you want to block in the list, you can also block it directly from the address bar when you visit that site. Look for a bell icon or a speaker icon in the address bar. If that icon is filled in or highlighted, the site currently has permission to send notifications. Click on the icon and select Block to revoke that permission immediately.

This method works particularly well if you just visited a new site and noticed it has notification permission that you did not intend to grant.

## Allowing Notifications From Trusted Sites

While you are in the notifications settings, you might also want to review which sites you still want to hear from. Some websites genuinely provide useful notifications, like a task management tool reminding you of deadlines or a calendar app alerting you to upcoming events.

Keep those permissions in place for sites that actually add value to your experience. The goal is not to turn off all notifications but to be intentional about which websites can interrupt you.

## Using the Block All Approach as a Baseline

If you find that too many websites have gotten notification permission over time, you might want to start fresh. In the same notifications settings area, you will see a toggle switch at the top that says "Sites can ask to send notifications." Turning this off prevents any website from asking for notification permission.

You can then selectively allow notifications only from the specific sites you trust. This gives you a clean slate and ensures that only websites you explicitly choose can send you alerts.

After blocking all requests, when you visit a new website, you will not see the notification prompt at all. If you decide later that you want to allow notifications from a particular site, you can always turn the global setting back on or manually add exceptions through the same settings page.

## What About Notifications From Extensions

Chrome extensions can also send notifications, and managing those is slightly different. To see which extensions have notification permissions, go back to Site settings and look for the Permissions section. You will see a list of permission types, and you can review which extensions have access to various features.

If an extension is sending you too many notifications, you can disable its permissions or remove the extension entirely. Sometimes extensions you installed for one purpose end up sending more alerts than you expected, so it is worth checking this periodically.

## Why This Matters for Browser Performance

Every notification that a website sends requires your browser to do a little extra work. When you have many sites with notification permission, your browser is constantly checking in the background to see if any of those sites want to alert you. This can slightly impact performance, especially on computers with limited resources.

By blocking notifications from sites you do not care about, you are not just reducing interruptions. You are also helping your browser run more efficiently. Fewer active notification connections mean Chrome can focus on the tabs you are actually using.

## Making This Part of Your Regular Browser Maintenance

Setting up your notification permissions is not a one-time task. As you visit new websites and install new extensions, your browser environment changes. Getting into the habit of reviewing your notification settings once a month or so can help you stay in control.

When a website asks for notification permission, take a quick moment to decide whether you really need alerts from that site. For sites you visit occasionally, clicking Block is usually the right choice. Only allow notifications from sites where you genuinely want to receive updates.

By following these steps and checking in on your settings regularly, you can enjoy the useful notifications while keeping the annoying ones at bay. Your browser should work for you, not against you.
