# Chrome Keeps Freezing Every Few Minutes: Causes and Fixes

Few things are more frustrating than using Chrome and having it freeze every few minutes. You are in the middle of reading an article, filling out a form, or watching a video, and suddenly everything locks up. The browser becomes unresponsive, tabs stop reacting, and you might even see the dreaded "page not responding" message. If this keeps happening repeatedly, it disrupts your workflow and makes browsing feel unreliable.

The good news is that Chrome freezing every few minutes is usually caused by a handful of common issues, and most of them have straightforward fixes. In this guide, I will walk you through the most likely reasons your browser keeps freezing and give you step-by-step solutions you can try right now.

## Why Does Chrome Keep Freezing?

Before jumping to fixes, it helps to understand what might be causing the problem. Chrome freezing every few minutes is typically related to one of these issues:

- **Too many open tabs** consuming memory
- **Outdated browser** or conflicting extensions
- **Cached data corruption**
- **Hardware acceleration conflicts**
- **Insufficient system resources**
- **Malware or unwanted software**

Let us look at each of these and how to address them.

## Fix 1: Close Unnecessary Tabs

This is the most common cause of Chrome freezing. Every tab you open runs processes in the background, using your computer's memory. When you have dozens of tabs open, Chrome has to juggle all of them, which can lead to freezing, especially on computers with limited RAM.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Look at your open tabs and close the ones you are not actively using
2. Consider using a tab management strategy, such as grouping related tabs or bookmarking pages you want to read later
3. If you frequently keep many tabs open, consider using an extension like **Tab Suspender Pro** to automatically suspend tabs you are not currently viewing. This frees up memory and can prevent Chrome from freezing every few minutes

After closing some tabs, test whether the freezing continues. If it stops, you have found the issue.

## Fix 2: Update Chrome

Running an outdated version of Chrome can cause performance issues, including freezing. Google regularly releases updates that include bug fixes, performance improvements, and security patches.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Open Chrome and click the three dots in the top-right corner
2. Hover over "Help" and select "About Google Chrome"
3. Chrome will automatically check for updates and install them if available
4. Restart your browser after the update completes

Keeping Chrome updated takes just a few minutes and often resolves freezing issues.

## Fix 3: Disable Problematic Extensions

Extensions are one of Chrome's most powerful features, but they can also cause problems. A poorly coded or outdated extension might interfere with Chrome's normal operation, causing it to freeze repeatedly. Sometimes a specific extension update can introduce bugs that affect performance.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Type `chrome://extensions` in the address bar and press Enter
2. Look for extensions you do not use frequently and click the "Remove" button to delete them
3. To identify a problematic extension, try disabling all extensions first: toggle off the "Developer mode" switch in the top-right, then reload Chrome and see if it still freezes
4. If the freezing stops with all extensions disabled, re-enable them one by one, testing after each one, until you find the culprit

Removing unused extensions not only fixes freezing but also improves overall browser speed.

## Fix 4: Clear Cache and Browsing Data

Over time, Chrome stores cached files, cookies, and other data to load websites faster. However, when this cached data becomes corrupted or grows too large, it can cause Chrome to freeze. Clearing this data is a quick fix that often works.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Press `Ctrl + Shift + Delete` (or `Cmd + Shift + Delete` on Mac) to open the clear browsing data window
2. Set the time range to "All time"
3. Check the boxes for "Cached images and files" and "Cookies and other site data"
4. Click "Clear data" and wait for the process to finish
5. Restart Chrome and test whether freezing continues

This clears out potentially problematic cached files and gives Chrome a fresh start.

## Fix 5: Disable Hardware Acceleration

Hardware acceleration allows Chrome to use your computer's GPU for certain tasks, which can improve performance. However, this feature can sometimes conflict with your graphics drivers or cause freezing, especially on older systems.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Click the three dots in the top-right corner and select "Settings"
2. Scroll down and click "Advanced" to expand more options
3. Under the "System" section, toggle off "Use hardware acceleration when available"
4. Restart Chrome and see if the freezing stops

If disabling hardware acceleration resolves the issue, you can leave it off. Your browser will still work, just using your CPU instead of your GPU for rendering.

## Fix 6: Check for Malware

Unwanted software or malware can interfere with Chrome and cause it to freeze. This includes browser hijackers, adwares, and other potentially unwanted programs that may have installed without your knowledge.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Run a full scan with your antivirus software
2. You can also use Chrome's built-in cleanup tool by typing `chrome://settings/cleanup` in the address bar and pressing Enter
3. Click "Find" to scan for harmful software
4. Remove any threats that are found and restart your computer

Keeping your system free of malware helps Chrome run smoothly and protects your personal data.

## Fix 7: Reset Chrome Settings

If none of the above fixes work, resetting Chrome to its default settings can resolve deeper configuration issues that might be causing freezing.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. Go to `chrome://settings/reset`
2. Click "Restore settings to their original defaults"
3. Confirm by clicking "Reset settings"
4. Restart Chrome

This will reset your startup pages, search engine, pinned tabs, and extensions. It often fixes persistent freezing issues.

## Fix 8: Check Your Computer's Resources

Sometimes the issue is not Chrome itself but your computer running low on resources. If your RAM is nearly full or your hard drive is almost full, Chrome may freeze when it cannot allocate enough memory.

**Step-by-step solution:**

1. On Windows, open Task Manager; on Mac, open Activity Monitor
2. Check your RAM usage. If it is above 90% consistently, close other applications
3. Ensure you have at least a few gigabytes of free storage space on your hard drive
4. Consider upgrading your RAM if you frequently run out of memory

Ensuring your system has enough resources lets Chrome run without competition.

## What To Do Next

If Chrome keeps freezing every few minutes, start with the simplest fixes first: close unnecessary tabs and clear your cache. These two steps resolve the majority of freezing issues. If the problem persists, work through the other solutions in order until you find the one that works.

Remember, an extension like **Tab Suspender Pro** can be a long-term solution if you tend to keep many tabs open. By automatically suspending inactive tabs, it helps prevent memory-related freezing and keeps your browsing experience smooth.
