# Chrome Notifications How to Stop Them

If you find yourself constantly interrupted by pop-ups asking for permission to send notifications, you are not alone. Many Chrome users ask themselves how to stop Chrome notifications from appearing and taking over their browsing experience. These notifications can be helpful in some situations, but they often become more of a distraction than a benefit. The good news is that turning them off is straightforward once you know where to look.

## Why Chrome Shows Notifications

Chrome notifications come from websites that you visit and have, at some point, asked for permission to send you updates. The first time you visit a news site, an online store, or a web application, you might see a pop-up asking if you want to allow notifications. Most people click "Allow" without thinking much about it, either because they want to receive updates or because they want to get past the prompt quickly.

Once you allow a website to send notifications, that site can push messages to your desktop even when you are not actively browsing that specific site. This can be useful for things like breaking news alerts or message notifications from communication tools. However, many websites abuse this feature by sending promotional messages, fake alerts, or unnecessary updates that clutter your screen and interrupt what you are doing.

Some websites also use notifications as a way to keep you coming back, sending you tempting offers or urgent messages that create a sense of FOMO. Over time, you might end up with dozens of sites all trying to get your attention through notifications, which defeats the purpose of having a clean browsing experience.

## Turn Off Notifications for Specific Websites

The most targeted approach is to disable notifications only for the sites that are bothering you while keeping them enabled for sites you actually want to hear from. Here is how to do that in Chrome.

Click on the lock icon or the site information icon to the left of the address bar on any webpage. This opens a small pop-up that shows you what permissions the site has. Look for the "Notifications" option and click on it. If it says "Allow," click on that and change it to "Block." This stops that specific website from sending you notifications.

You can also manage these permissions more comprehensively by typing "chrome://settings/content/notifications" into your address bar and pressing Enter. This page shows you a list of all websites that are allowed to send notifications. You can go through the list and click the three dots next to any site you want to change, then select "Block" to remove that site's notification privileges.

## Disable All Notifications in Chrome Settings

If you find that too many sites are sending you notifications and you would rather turn them off entirely, Chrome has a global setting that stops all websites from asking for or sending notifications.

Open Chrome and click the three dots in the top right corner to access the menu. Select "Settings" from the dropdown. On the settings page, type "notifications" in the search bar at the top. Click on "Notifications" in the search results. You will see an option that says "Sites can ask to send notifications." Simply toggle this switch to the off position.

Turning this off means no website will be able to send you notifications, and you will no longer see those annoying pop-ups asking for permission. If you ever need notifications from a specific site in the future, you can always turn this setting back on and then allow that particular site manually.

## Manage Notifications on Desktop

Chrome notifications can also appear as desktop notifications, which show up in the corner of your computer screen even when Chrome is not in focus. These are separate from the browser settings and are controlled by your operating system.

On Windows, click the Start menu and type "Notifications settings" to open the Windows notification center. Look for Chrome in the list of apps that can send notifications and turn it off.

On Mac, open System Settings and click on "Notifications." Find Chrome in the app list and disable notifications there. This ensures that even if a website manages to get past Chrome's internal blocking, the notification will not appear on your desktop.

## Check Your Extensions

Sometimes the source of annoying notifications is not the websites themselves but an extension you have installed. Some extensions use notifications to alert you about deals, price changes, social media activity, or other updates. If you have many extensions installed, one of them might be the culprit behind notification overload.

To check your extensions, type "chrome://extensions" in the address bar and press Enter. Review each extension you have installed. If you notice any extension that you do not use regularly or that seems to be sending you too many notifications, click the "Remove" button to delete it entirely.

For extensions you want to keep, click the "Details" button to open the extension's settings. Look for any options related to notifications and disable them if you do not want to receive alerts from that extension.

## Review and Clean Up Regularly

The best way to keep notifications under control is to periodically review which sites have permission to send them. Set a reminder to check your notification settings once a month and remove permissions for sites you no longer visit or care about.

When a website asks for permission to send notifications, take a moment to think about whether you really need updates from that site. If the answer is no, click "Block" instead of "Allow." This simple habit will prevent notification buildup over time and keep your browsing experience much calmer.
