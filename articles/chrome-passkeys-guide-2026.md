# Chrome Passkeys Guide 2026

Passwords have been the standard for online security for decades, but they are increasingly becoming a thing of the past. If you have ever forgotten a password, struggled to come up with a secure one, or worried about your accounts being breached, passkeys offer a better way. Google Chrome has made significant strides in supporting passkeys, and 2026 is the year many users are making the switch. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know about using passkeys in Chrome, from creating them to managing them across your devices.

## What Are Passkeys and Why Should You Care

Passkeys are a modern authentication method that replaces traditional passwords entirely. Instead of typing a string of characters that you have to remember and keep secure, passkeys use cryptographic keys that are stored on your device. When you want to log into a website, you simply authenticate using your fingerprint, face scan, or device PIN. This could be through your phone, computer, or even a security key.

The reason passkeys are better than passwords is rooted in how they work. Passwords can be guessed, stolen, leaked in data breaches, or phished through fake websites. Passkeys, on the other hand, are tied to specific websites and cannot be used on fraudulent sites. Even if a website suffers a data breach, your passkey cannot be used to access your account because the cryptographic key never leaves your device. This makes passkeys significantly more secure than even the strongest passwords.

From a practical standpoint, passkeys are also much more convenient. You do not need to think of a unique password for every account, remember which one you used where, or deal with password reset emails. You just log in the way you would unlock your phone, and you are in. For Chrome users, this experience is now available on both desktop and mobile devices, making it a viable replacement for passwords across almost all your accounts.

## How to Create Passkeys in Chrome

Creating a passkey in Chrome is a straightforward process, though it requires that the website you want to use supports passkeys. Major platforms like Google, Apple, Microsoft, PayPal, Amazon, and many others now support passkey authentication, with more websites adding support every month.

To create a passkey on a desktop computer using Chrome, start by visiting a website that supports passkeys and logging into your account as you normally would. Once you are logged in, go to your account or security settings. Look for options labeled Passkeys, Passwordless Login, or Add Security Key. The exact wording varies by website, but it is usually found under Security, Login, or Account settings.

When you select the option to create a passkey, Chrome will prompt you to choose where to store it. You can save it to your computer if you are using a Mac or Windows machine, or you can save it to your phone if you have Chrome set up to sync with your mobile device. You will then authenticate using your device's biometric system, such as Touch ID on a Mac or Windows Hello on a Windows PC. This could involve scanning your fingerprint, using facial recognition, or entering your device PIN.

On an Android phone, the process is similar. Open Chrome, navigate to the website, log in, and go to the security settings. When you create a passkey, you will be prompted to use your fingerprint or device PIN to confirm. The passkey will then be stored securely on your Android device, protected by the phone's hardware-backed security.

One important thing to understand is that passkeys are tied to both the specific website and the specific device or account where you create them. This means you will need to create a passkey on each device you want to use, or you can choose to sync your passkeys through your Google account, which brings us to the next section.

## Syncing Passkeys Across Your Devices

One of the biggest concerns people have when switching to passkeys is whether they will be locked to a single device. The good news is that Chrome makes it easy to sync your passkeys across all your devices using your Google account. This means you can create a passkey on your laptop and then use it to log in on your phone, or vice versa.

To enable passkey syncing, make sure you are signed into Chrome with your Google account on all your devices. When you create a passkey, Chrome will ask if you want to save it to your Google account. Choosing this option will make the passkey available on any device where you are signed into the same Google account and have Chrome set up to sync.

This sync feature is particularly useful because it means you do not need to create separate passkeys for each of your devices. You create one passkey for a website, and it becomes available everywhere. This dramatically simplifies the process of going passwordless while still giving you the flexibility to log in from any of your devices.

It is worth noting that passkeys stored in your Google account are encrypted and protected by Google's security infrastructure. They are not accessible by Google itself, and the company cannot use them to log into your accounts. The encryption keys are stored only on your trusted devices, ensuring that your passkeys remain secure even if Google's servers were somehow compromised.

For users who prefer not to sync through Google, there are alternatives. You can create passkeys on individual devices and use them locally. Some password managers also support storing passkeys, giving you another way to keep them organized and accessible across devices. However, for most Chrome users, syncing through Google offers the best balance of convenience and security.

## Using Passkeys to Log In

Once you have created a passkey for a website, using it is remarkably simple. The next time you visit that website and navigate to the login page, Chrome will automatically detect that a passkey is available for that site. A prompt will appear asking if you want to use your passkey to log in.

When you click to use the passkey, you will be asked to authenticate using your device's biometric system or PIN. On a desktop computer, this might involve using Touch ID on a MacBook, Windows Hello on a Windows machine, or entering your device PIN. On mobile, you would use your fingerprint, face scan, or PIN. Once authenticated, you are logged in instantly without typing anything.

Chrome also supports a feature called conditional mediation, which means the passkey prompt will appear automatically on the login page in some cases. This makes the login process feel seamless, almost like the website is just remembering you. The experience is noticeably faster than typing a password, especially on mobile where entering complex passwords on a small keyboard can be tedious.

If you have multiple passkeys for the same website, perhaps because you created one on your computer and another on your phone, Chrome will let you choose which one to use. You can also set a default passkey for each site if you want to streamline the process further.

One thing to keep in mind is that passkeys only work on websites that support them. While support is growing rapidly, you will still encounter some sites that require traditional passwords. In those cases, Chrome can still help by suggesting and storing strong passwords, which you can use as a fallback. Over time, as more websites adopt passkey support, you will find yourself needing passwords less and less.

## Replacing Your Passwords with Passkeys

Making the switch from passwords to passkeys is a gradual process. You do not need to convert all your accounts overnight. Instead, you can start with your most important accounts, such as your primary email, banking, and shopping sites, and work your way through the rest over time.

When you are ready to replace a password with a passkey, start by logging into the website using your existing password. Then, go to the security settings and look for the option to add a passkey as a login method. Some websites will even prompt you to create a passkey when you log in, making the process even easier.

After creating a passkey, you can keep your password as a backup for a while, just in case. However, once you are confident that the passkey is working well, you can delete the password from Chrome's stored passwords or from the website itself. This reduces the attack surface because passwords are the weak link in most account breaches.

For websites that do not yet support passkeys, continue using Chrome's password manager to generate and store strong, unique passwords. Chrome can alert you if your saved passwords have been exposed in a data breach, helping you stay ahead of potential security issues. As more websites add passkey support, you will be able to gradually phase out passwords entirely.

It is also a good idea to review your accounts periodically and prioritize converting the ones that hold the most sensitive information. Email accounts, in particular, are worth prioritizing because they often serve as the recovery method for other accounts. Securing your email with a passkey adds an extra layer of protection to your entire online identity.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of Passkeys

While passkeys are designed to be simple, there are a few things you can do to ensure the best experience. First, make sure Chrome is updated to the latest version. Google regularly releases updates that improve passkey functionality, fix bugs, and add support for new websites. Keeping Chrome updated ensures you have access to the latest features.

Second, enable biometric authentication on your devices if available. Whether it is Touch ID on a Mac, Windows Hello on a PC, or the fingerprint scanner on your Android phone, using biometrics makes the passkey login process fast and seamless. It also adds an extra layer of security because the passkey is tied to your physical biometric data.

Third, if you use multiple Google accounts, be aware that passkeys are tied to the account you were signed into when you created them. Make sure you create passkeys using the correct account, especially for important sites. You can manage which accounts have passkeys stored by visiting your Google Account settings.

Fourth, consider using a backup authentication method for critical accounts. While passkeys are extremely secure, it is still wise to have a backup way to access your account in case you lose your device or cannot use your biometric authentication. This might be a backup email, phone number, or in some cases, a physical security key.

Finally, remember that passkeys do not eliminate the need for good overall security practices. Continue to be cautious about suspicious emails and links, keep your software updated, and use Chrome's other security features. Passkeys solve the password problem, but they are part of a broader approach to staying safe online.

## Managing Passkeys and Browser Performance

As you start using passkeys and accumulating more extensions and features in Chrome, you may notice your browser using more memory, especially if you keep many tabs open. This is where thoughtful tab management becomes important. Having dozens of open tabs not only slows down your browser but can also increase security risks by keeping sessions active on sites you no longer need.

If you find yourself with many open tabs, consider using tools that help you manage them efficiently. Tab Suspender Pro, for example, automatically suspends tabs you are not actively using, freeing up memory and keeping your browser running smoothly. It also gives you a clear overview of which tabs are open, making it easier to stay organized. This is especially helpful as you transition to using passkeys and may find yourself logging into more sites than before, leading to more open tabs.

Combining good tab management with passkeys creates a more pleasant browsing experience. You get the security and convenience of passwordless login, the speed of a well-organized browser, and the peace of mind that comes from knowing your accounts are better protected.

## The Future of Passwordless Authentication

Passkeys represent the biggest change in online authentication since the password was invented. In 2026, they have moved from an experimental feature to a practical, widely-supported standard that offers real advantages over passwords. Major websites, browsers, and operating systems have embraced passkeys, creating an ecosystem where passwordless login is increasingly the norm rather than the exception.

As a Chrome user, you are in an excellent position to take advantage of this shift. Chrome's implementation of passkeys is robust, well-integrated, and easy to use. By creating passkeys for your most important accounts, syncing them across your devices, and gradually replacing passwords, you can significantly improve both your security and your daily browsing experience.

The transition does not happen overnight, but every passkey you create is a step toward a more secure, more convenient online life. Start with one account today, see how easy it is, and you will likely find yourself converting more accounts in the weeks and months to come. Passkeys are not just the future of authentication; they are the present, and Chrome makes it easier than ever to join the passwordless revolution.
