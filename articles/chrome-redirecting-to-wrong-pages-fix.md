Chrome redirecting to wrong pages fix is something many people search for when their browser suddenly starts sending them to websites they never intended to visit. You type one address in the address bar, press enter, and somehow you land on a completely different page. This can be confusing and annoying, and it might make you wonder what is going on with your browser.

The good news is that this problem is almost always fixable without any technical expertise. In most cases, Chrome redirecting to wrong pages happens because of a few common issues that you can check and correct on your own.

## What Causes Chrome to Redirect to Wrong Pages

Several things can cause Chrome to send you to the wrong website. Understanding what causes the problem makes it easier to fix.

One of the most common causes is a browser hijacker. This is a type of extension or program that takes control of your browser settings without proper permission. Browser hijackers often change your default search engine or homepage to one that makes money for the people who created the hijacker. When you try to search or visit a site, you get redirected to their chosen destinations instead.

Another cause is problematic extensions. Sometimes you install an extension that seems helpful but actually has hidden behaviors. These extensions might redirect your links, change your search results, or send you to advertising pages. The extension might have started behaving this way after an update, or it might have been this way all along and you only just noticed.

Your DNS settings can also cause redirect problems. DNS is like a phone book for the internet that translates website names into numbers your computer can understand. If these settings get changed or corrupted, you might end up at the wrong website even when typing the correct address.

Sometimes the problem comes from cached data in Chrome. Your browser saves information about websites to load them faster next time. If this cached information gets mixed up or outdated, it can cause redirect issues.

Finally, malicious websites can cause redirects too. Some websites try to exploit browser vulnerabilities or trick you into clicking something that sends you elsewhere. These sites might redirect you multiple times, trying to get you to download something harmful or visit a scam page.

## How to Fix Chrome Redirecting to Wrong Pages

You can work through several steps to fix this problem. Try these in order, and check if the issue is resolved after each step.

### Check Your Extensions

Start by looking at what extensions you have installed. Click the puzzle piece icon in Chrome and select Manage Extensions. Look through the list and think about when the redirects started happening.

If you see any extension you do not recognize or do not remember installing, remove it. Also remove any extension that seems suspicious, even if you installed it yourself. Sometimes extensions get updated and start behaving differently.

To remove an extension, click the three dots next to it and select Remove from Chrome. Confirm when asked.

### Reset Your Search Engine

If your searches are being redirected, your default search engine might have been changed. Click the three dots in Chrome, select Settings, and then click Search engine on the left side.

Make sure your search engine is set to Google, Bing, or another reputable option. If it is set to something you do not recognize, click the dropdown and select a proper search engine.

You might also want to manage your search engines by clicking Search engine management and removing any unfamiliar ones from the list.

### Clear Your Browser Cache and Cookies

Cached data can sometimes cause redirect problems. To clear this, click the three dots in Chrome, select Clear browsing data. Choose a time range like All time to be thorough.

Make sure the boxes for Cookies and other site data and Cached images and files are checked. Then click Clear data.

This will sign you out of some websites, so you might need to log in again afterward.

### Check Your DNS Settings

If the problem persists, your computer's DNS settings might need attention. On Windows, search for Network and Internet settings, click Network and Sharing Center, then change adapter settings. Right-click your active network, select Properties, double-click Internet Protocol Version 4, and make sure it is set to Obtain DNS server address automatically.

On Mac, go to System Settings, click Network, select your active connection, click Details, and make sure DNS is set to automatic.

If you are not comfortable changing these settings, you can skip this step and try the next one instead.

### Reset Chrome Settings

If nothing else has worked, you can reset Chrome to its original settings. This removes extensions, resets your search engine and homepage, and clears cookies and site data. Your bookmarks and saved passwords will not be deleted.

To do this, go to Settings, click Advanced on the left, and select Reset and clean up. Click Restore settings to their original defaults, then confirm by clicking Reset settings.

## Preventing Redirect Problems in the Future

Once you have fixed the redirect issue, you will want to keep it from happening again.

Be careful about what extensions you install. Only add extensions from developers you trust, and read reviews before installing. If an extension asks for permission to access data on all websites, consider whether that makes sense for what the extension does.

Keep Chrome updated. New versions include security fixes that protect against malicious websites and redirect attempts. Chrome usually updates automatically, but you can check by clicking the three dots and selecting Help, then About Google Chrome.

Be cautious when installing other software on your computer. Some free programs try to change your browser settings during installation. Always choose custom installation if possible and watch for any offers to change your homepage or search engine.
