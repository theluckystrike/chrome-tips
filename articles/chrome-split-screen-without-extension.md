# Chrome Split Screen Without Extension

Chrome split screen without extension is something many people search for when they want to view two websites side by side without adding extra tools to their browser. Whether you are comparing products, researching information, or working on documents, seeing two pages at once can save you a lot of time and clicking.

The good news is that Chrome has built-in ways to achieve split screen functionality without installing any extensions. You can arrange your windows to see two tabs side by side using simple techniques that work on both Windows and Mac computers.

## Why Use Split Screen in Chrome

There are many situations where seeing two websites at once makes sense. When you are shopping online, you might want to compare prices across different stores. When you are writing research papers, you may need to read source material while taking notes in another document. When you are following a recipe, you could have the instructions on one side and a measurement converter on the other.

Without split screen, you constantly switch between tabs, which breaks your focus and slows you down. Having both pages visible at the same time keeps your workflow smooth and helps you complete tasks faster.

## Using Window Snapping for Split Screen

The easiest way to get chrome split screen without extension is to use your operating system's window snapping feature. This method works on both Windows and Mac, though the exact gestures differ slightly.

On Windows, click and hold on the Chrome window title bar, then drag it to the left side of your screen. When you see a shaded area appear, release your mouse, and Chrome will snap to fill exactly half of your screen. Then open a second Chrome window and drag it to the right side. Now you have two Chrome windows side by side, each displaying a different tab.

You can adjust the width of each window by moving the divider between them. This gives you complete control over how much screen space each page gets.

On Mac, you can use the window tiling feature. Click and hold on the green maximize button in the Chrome window, then drag to the left or right side of your screen. Release to snap the window to that half. Do the same with a second window on the opposite side.

This approach is perfect for anyone who wants chrome split screen without extension because it uses features built into your computer rather than adding extra software to your browser.

## Using Tab Tiling Within Chrome

Chrome also offers a way to tile tabs within a single window. This is useful when you want to keep everything organized in one place.

To try this, right-click on a tab and select "Move tab to new window." Then, drag the new window next to your original Chrome window using the snapping method described above. This creates two separate windows that you can arrange side by side.

While Chrome does not have a one-click "split view" button built into the interface, combining tab management with window snapping gives you the same result. You end up with two windows taking up half the screen each, just like a true split screen setup.

One thing to note is that this method keeps your tabs in separate windows. If you prefer keeping all tabs in one window, you might find this less convenient than other approaches.

## Using Chrome Side Panel

Chrome includes a side panel feature that can help with split screen tasks, though it works differently from traditional split screen. The side panel lets you view a second page alongside your main content.

To use the side panel, click the icon that looks like a square with a line on the right edge of your toolbar. This opens a panel on the right side of your browser where you can browse a different website while keeping your main page visible.

This is helpful for looking up information quickly without losing your place on the main page. However, the side panel is narrower than a true split screen, so it works better for reference lookups than for detailed comparison work.

## When an Extension Might Help

While chrome split screen without extension is definitely possible, you might find that an extension makes certain tasks easier. For example, if you frequently compare multiple pages or need more flexible arrangements, extensions can save you time.

Tab Suspender Pro is one option worth considering. It helps manage your tabs by suspending ones you are not actively using, which keeps Chrome running smoothly when you have many tabs open. This is particularly useful if you tend to keep many pages available for reference while working. The extension handles tab management automatically, so you can focus on your work rather than browser performance.

Extensions like Tab Suspender Pro are not required for split screen, but they can enhance your workflow if you find yourself frequently working with multiple pages open.

## Tips for Effective Split Screen Use

Making the most of split screen in Chrome takes some practice. Here are some suggestions to help you work efficiently.

Think about which page should go on which side. Most people find it natural to keep the page they are actively working on on the left and the reference material on the right. This setup reduces the need to move your eyes across the entire screen constantly.

Use keyboard shortcuts to switch between windows quickly. Alt-tab on Windows and Command-tab on Mac let you jump between applications, while Ctrl-tab within Chrome switches between tabs in your current window.

Consider your monitor size. Larger monitors give you more room to work with both pages comfortably. On smaller screens, you might need to zoom out slightly on each page to see enough content.

Keep your windows organized. If you frequently use specific pairs of websites together, consider keeping them in separate windows that you can quickly arrange side by side.

## Final Thoughts

Getting chrome split screen without extension is straightforward once you know the techniques. Window snapping on both Windows and Mac provides a reliable way to view two pages side by side without adding any extensions to your browser.

For simple split screen needs, the built-in methods work well and require no additional software. For more complex workflows or frequent multi-tasking, you can explore options like Tab Suspender Pro to enhance your experience.

The best approach is the one that fits your specific needs. Try the different methods described here and see which one works best for your daily tasks.

## Using Your Computer's Window Snapping

The simplest way to get Chrome split screen without extension is to use the window management features already built into your computer. This approach works with any application, not just Chrome, and requires no special browser settings.

On Windows, start by opening your first Chrome window. Click and hold the title bar at the top of the window, then drag it toward the left side of your screen. When you see a shaded area appear covering half the screen, release your mouse and the window will snap into place. Now open a second Chrome window and drag it to the right side of your screen until it fills the remaining half. You now have two Chrome windows side by side, each displaying a different website. On Mac, you can achieve the same result using a feature called window tiling. Open a Chrome window and hover over the green fullscreen button in the top left corner. You will see options appear to tile the window to the left or right side of your screen. Click on one of these options, then open another Chrome window and tile it to the opposite side. This gives you the same side by side split screen effect without needing any extensions.

If you are using Linux, most desktop environments include similar window snapping features. Check your system settings or simply drag windows to the edges of your screen to discover what options are available.

## Using Chrome's Vertical Tabs Feature

Chrome includes a built-in feature called vertical tabs that can help you organize your browser windows in ways that make split screen easier to manage. While this is not exactly a split screen feature, it complements the methods described here and helps you see more of your tab bar without taking up valuable screen space.

To enable vertical tabs, right-click on any tab in your Chrome window and select "Set up vertical tabs" from the menu. Your tab bar will move to the left side of the browser window, becoming narrower and displaying tabs vertically. This frees up more horizontal space for the websites you are viewing, making it easier to arrange windows side by side without feeling cramped.

Vertical tabs are particularly helpful when you are working with multiple projects at once. You can create different tab groups for different tasks and collapse groups you are not currently using. When you need to view two websites side by side, you already have more screen real estate available because your tabs are not taking up space at the top of the window.

## Splitting Tabs Within One Chrome Window

Chrome also allows you to split tabs within a single browser window, though this works differently than traditional split screen. You can create a new window from any tab by right-clicking on the tab and selecting "Move to new window." This essentially ungroups the tab from your current window and places it in its own separate window, which you can then arrange side by side with your original window using the window snapping methods described earlier.

This approach gives you more flexibility because each window operates independently. You can minimize one without affecting the other, and closing one window does not accidentally close your other tabs. It also means you can use different Chrome profiles for different tasks if needed, keeping your work and personal browsing separate.

Another built-in approach involves using Chrome's tab groups alongside your split screen setup. Right-click on any tab to create a new group, give it a name and color, and then add related tabs to that group. When you are using split screen to view two websites, tab groups help you keep track of which tabs belong to which project, even when they are not visible on screen.

Extensions become valuable when you have specific workflow requirements that go beyond what the browser offers natively.

Another tip is to adjust the zoom level in each Chrome window. You do not need both sides of your split screen to have the same zoom level. If one website needs larger text for comfortable reading, use the zoom controls in Chrome to increase it without affecting the other side. Access zoom controls by clicking the three dots in the top right corner of Chrome and using the plus and minus buttons, or simply hold Control and use your mouse wheel to adjust quickly.

You can also use Chrome's picture-in-picture feature alongside split screen if you need to watch a video while working on something else.
