# Chrome Split Screen Without Extension

Chrome split screen without extension is something many people search for when they want to view two websites side by side without adding extra tools to their browser. Whether you are comparing products, researching information, or working on documents, seeing two pages at once can save you a lot of time and clicking.

The good news is that Chrome has built-in ways to achieve split screen functionality without installing any extensions. You can arrange your windows to see two tabs side by side using simple techniques that work on both Windows and Mac computers.

## Why Use Split Screen in Chrome

There are many situations where seeing two websites at once makes sense. When you are shopping online, you might want to compare prices across different stores. When you are writing research papers, you may need to read source material while taking notes in another document. When you are following a recipe, you could have the instructions on one side and a measurement converter on the other.

Without split screen, you constantly switch between tabs, which breaks your focus and slows you down. Having both pages visible at the same time keeps your workflow smooth and helps you complete tasks faster.

## Using Window Snapping for Split Screen

The easiest way to get chrome split screen without extension is to use your operating system's window snapping feature. This method works on both Windows and Mac, though the exact gestures differ slightly.

On Windows, click and hold on the Chrome window title bar, then drag it to the left side of your screen. When you see a shaded area appear, release your mouse, and Chrome will snap to fill exactly half of your screen. Then open a second Chrome window and drag it to the right side. Now you have two Chrome windows side by side, each displaying a different tab.

You can adjust the width of each window by moving the divider between them. This gives you complete control over how much screen space each page gets.

On Mac, you can use the window tiling feature. Click and hold on the green maximize button in the Chrome window, then drag to the left or right side of your screen. Release to snap the window to that half. Do the same with a second window on the opposite side.

This approach is perfect for anyone who wants chrome split screen without extension because it uses features built into your computer rather than adding extra software to your browser.

## Using Tab Tiling Within Chrome

Chrome also offers a way to tile tabs within a single window. This is useful when you want to keep everything organized in one place.

To try this, right-click on a tab and select "Move tab to new window." Then, drag the new window next to your original Chrome window using the snapping method described above. This creates two separate windows that you can arrange side by side.

While Chrome does not have a one-click "split view" button built into the interface, combining tab management with window snapping gives you the same result. You end up with two windows taking up half the screen each, just like a true split screen setup.

One thing to note is that this method keeps your tabs in separate windows. If you prefer keeping all tabs in one window, you might find this less convenient than other approaches.

## Using Chrome Side Panel

Chrome includes a side panel feature that can help with split screen tasks, though it works differently from traditional split screen. The side panel lets you view a second page alongside your main content.

To use the side panel, click the icon that looks like a square with a line on the right edge of your toolbar. This opens a panel on the right side of your browser where you can browse a different website while keeping your main page visible.

This is helpful for looking up information quickly without losing your place on the main page. However, the side panel is narrower than a true split screen, so it works better for reference lookups than for detailed comparison work.

Extensions like Tab Suspender Pro are not required for split screen, but they can enhance your workflow if you find yourself frequently working with multiple pages open.

## Tips for Effective Split Screen Use

Making the most of split screen in Chrome takes some practice. Here are some suggestions to help you work efficiently.

Think about which page should go on which side. Most people find it natural to keep the page they are actively working on on the left and the reference material on the right. This setup reduces the need to move your eyes across the entire screen constantly.

Use keyboard shortcuts to switch between windows quickly. Alt-tab on Windows and Command-tab on Mac let you jump between applications, while Ctrl-tab within Chrome switches between tabs in your current window.

Consider your monitor size. Larger monitors give you more room to work with both pages comfortably. On smaller screens, you might need to zoom out slightly on each page to see enough content.

Keep your windows organized. If you frequently use specific pairs of websites together, consider keeping them in separate windows that you can quickly arrange side by side.
