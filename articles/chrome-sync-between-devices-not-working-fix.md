# Chrome Sync Between Devices Not Working Fix

Chrome sync between devices not working is one of those frustrating issues that can completely disrupt your workflow. You bookmark an interesting article on your laptop, expecting it to be waiting for you on your phone during your commute. But when you open Chrome on your phone, the bookmark is nowhere to be found. Your passwords, history, and open tabs should all be there, but they are not. This happens more often than you might think, and there are several reasons why it occurs. The good news is that most of the time, you can fix it yourself without needing technical help.

## Why Chrome Sync Between Devices Stops Working

Chrome sync is designed to keep your browsing data synchronized across every device where you use the same Google account. This includes your bookmarks, browsing history, saved passwords, autofill data, extensions, and even your open tabs. When it works correctly, you can start researching something on your desktop computer and continue exactly where you left off on your phone. But several factors can break this seamless experience.

The most common reason chrome sync between devices not working occurs is a mismatch in the Google account you are using. If you signed into Chrome with your personal Gmail account on your computer but accidentally used a different Google account on your phone, the two devices are essentially talking past each other. They are both syncing, just to different places. This is surprisingly easy to do, especially if you have multiple Google accounts.

Another frequent cause is having sync itself disabled. Sometimes this happens by accident when you are tweaking settings or after a browser update resets your preferences. You might also find that sync is paused, which Chrome does automatically when it detects you have been inactive for a while to save data and battery life.

Network issues can also interfere with sync functionality. If your internet connection is unstable or you are behind a corporate firewall that blocks Google's servers, Chrome will not be able to upload or download your data. Similarly, if you have disabled cookies or cleared them recently, this can prevent sync from working properly since Chrome uses cookies to authenticate your connection to the sync servers.

Browser data corruption is another culprit. Over time, the local files that Chrome uses to manage sync can become damaged or outdated. When this happens, Chrome might try to sync but end up sending corrupted data or nothing at all. This is particularly common if you have not updated Chrome in a while or if your computer has been shut down unexpectedly.

Finally, there is the matter of sync settings. Even when sync is turned on, individual categories of data might be disabled. You could be syncing your passwords but not your bookmarks, which creates the confusing impression that sync is only partially working.

## How to Fix Chrome Sync Between Devices Not Working

Let us walk through the solutions from simplest to most thorough. Try these in order and you should find the fix without needing to dig too deep.

### Make Sure You Are Signed Into the Same Google Account

This sounds obvious, but it is the most common cause of sync problems. Open Chrome on your computer and click your profile picture in the top right corner. Look at which account is currently signed in. Now check your phone, tablet, and any other devices. All of them need to be using the same Google email address for the data to flow between them.

If you discover a mismatch, sign out of the incorrect account on each device and sign in with the correct one. When you do this, Chrome will typically ask whether you want to merge the existing data on that device with your synced data or replace it entirely. Choose merge if you want to keep both sets of bookmarks and passwords.

### Check That Sync Is Actually Turned On

After confirming you are using the correct account, make sure sync is enabled. In Chrome on your computer, click your profile picture and look for the sync icon, which looks like two arrows forming a circle. If it is grayed out or has a line through it, click it and select Turn On Sync.

Once you have turned sync on, give it a few minutes to start working. You should see the sync icon become active, which indicates that Chrome is now uploading your data to your Google account. Check your other devices to see if the data has appeared.

### Verify Which Sync Items Are Enabled

Even when sync is turned on, individual data categories might be turned off. Click your profile picture in Chrome and select Sync. You will see a list of items including Bookmarks, History, Passwords, Autofill, Open Tabs, and Settings. Make sure every item you want to sync across devices is toggled on.

It is worth checking this on each device because different devices might have different items enabled. For example, you might have turned off history sync on your phone to save data but forgotten about it.

### Restart Chrome and Your Devices

Sometimes the simplest solution is the most effective. Close Chrome completely on your computer and then reopen it. On Windows, make sure the browser is not still running in the background by right-clicking the Chrome icon in your taskbar and selecting Quit. On Mac, right-click the Chrome icon in your dock and select Quit.

After restarting Chrome, check whether sync has started working. You can also try restarting your phone or tablet, as this can help establish a fresh connection to Google's sync servers.

### Check Your Internet Connection

Sync requires a stable internet connection to work properly. If your Wi-Fi is acting up or you have a weak signal, Chrome will not be able to sync your data. Try resetting your router or switching to a different network.

If you are on a corporate or school network, check whether the administrators have blocked access to Google's sync services. You might need to use a personal network or a VPN to get sync working in that environment. You can test this by opening a new tab and loading a few websites to confirm your connection is working.

### Update Chrome

Make sure you are running the latest version of Chrome on all your devices. Outdated versions can have bugs that affect sync functionality. To check for updates, open Chrome Settings and click About Chrome. Let it check for updates and install any that are available, then restart the browser.

### Clear Sync Data and Start Fresh

If the problem persists, the local sync data in Chrome might be corrupted. You can reset this by going to Chrome Settings, clicking Advanced, and selecting Reset Settings. Choose Restore settings to their original defaults. This will not delete your bookmarks or passwords from your Google account, but it will clear the local copy and force Chrome to download everything fresh.

Alternatively, you can go to your Google Account settings, find the Chrome Sync section, and choose to stop sync and clear data from this device. Then turn sync back on and let Chrome re-download everything.

### Check for Conflicting Extensions

Some Chrome extensions can interfere with sync functionality. Try disabling all your extensions temporarily and see if sync starts working. If it does, re-enable the extensions one by one until you find the culprit. Extensions that modify browser settings or handle data in unusual ways are the most likely suspects.

## Keeping Sync Working Smoothly

Once you get sync working again, a few habits will help keep it stable. Try not to switch between different Google accounts frequently, as this can cause conflicts and duplicate data. Keep Chrome updated on all your devices so you always have the latest features and bug fixes. And check occasionally that all your devices are still signed into the same account and that sync is still enabled.

Chrome sync between devices is incredibly useful when it works, and most of the time it does work without you even thinking about it. When it breaks, it is usually one of the simple issues we covered here, and now you have the steps to fix it.
