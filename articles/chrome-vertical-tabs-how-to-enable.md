# Chrome Vertical Tabs How to Enable

If you have ever felt like your Chrome browser is running out of horizontal space because you have too many tabs open at once, you are not alone. Many people struggle with the traditional horizontal tab bar at the top of their browser window. When you open more than a handful of tabs, they start getting smaller, harder to read, and sometimes you cannot even see the titles anymore. This makes it difficult to find the specific tab you need, and it can feel overwhelming when you are trying to work efficiently. The good news is that Chrome has a feature called vertical tabs that can solve this problem. If you have been searching for "chrome vertical tabs how to enable," this guide will walk you through the process step by step.

## Why Vertical Tabs Matter

Chrome vertical tabs allow you to display your open tabs in a sidebar on the left side of the browser window instead of the traditional horizontal row at the top. This simple change can make a huge difference in how you manage your browsing experience. When tabs are arranged vertically, each tab shows its full title clearly, making it much easier to scan through them and find exactly what you are looking for. You can also see more tabs at once without scrolling, which saves time and reduces frustration.

The horizontal tab layout works fine when you have a few tabs open, but it becomes problematic as soon as you start working with many pages simultaneously. Researchers, developers, writers, and anyone who juggles multiple projects online often find themselves with dozens of tabs open. In that scenario, vertical tabs can transform a cluttered mess into an organized system that actually helps you stay productive.

Another advantage of vertical tabs is that they work seamlessly with Chrome's side panel feature. You can keep your tabs organized while still having plenty of room for the actual web content you are viewing. It is a cleaner, more modern approach to browser navigation that many users have been asking for.

## How to Enable Vertical Tabs in Chrome

Chrome introduced vertical tabs as a built-in feature, but it may not be turned on by default. Here is how you can enable it.

First, make sure you are using the latest version of Chrome. Open Chrome and click on the three dots in the top right corner of the window. Select "Settings" from the dropdown menu. In the settings page, look for the "Appearance" section on the left sidebar. Click on it, and you should see an option called "Vertical tabs." Toggle the switch to turn it on. Once enabled, Chrome will display your tabs in a collapsible sidebar on the left side of the window.

If you do not see the option in Settings, you might need to enable it through Chrome Flags, which is a page that contains experimental features. Type "chrome://flags" in your address bar and press Enter. In the search box on the page, type "vertical tabs." You should see an option labeled "Vertical Tabs." Click on the dropdown next to it and select "Enabled." Chrome will ask you to relaunch the browser for the changes to take effect. After restarting, the vertical tabs feature should be available in your settings.

When vertical tabs are enabled, you will see a new icon in your toolbar that looks like a sideways arrow or a sidebar icon. Click on it to open or close the vertical tabs panel. You can drag and drop tabs to reorder them, and you can also drag tabs out of the sidebar to open them in a new window if needed.

## Customizing Your Vertical Tabs Experience

Once you have vertical tabs enabled, you can customize how they look and behave to suit your preferences. Chrome offers a few different options in the settings. You can choose whether the vertical tabs panel should stay open by default or collapse automatically when you are not using it. You can also set the width of the sidebar to be narrower or wider depending on how much screen space you want to allocate to your tab list.

If you prefer a more compact view, you can choose to show only favicons without the full tab titles. This gives you the benefits of vertical organization while still seeing which sites each tab belongs to. On the other hand, if you want maximum readability, you can expand the sidebar to show full titles and even preview snippets of the page content when you hover over a tab.

The vertical tabs feature also integrates well with Chrome's tab groups. You can create color-coded groups for different projects or topics, and these groups will appear in the vertical sidebar, making it even easier to navigate between related tabs. This combination of vertical layout and tab groups gives you powerful tools for staying organized.

## Using Extensions for More Features

While Chrome's built-in vertical tabs are a great starting point, some users want additional features and customization options. There are several Chrome extensions available that can enhance the vertical tabs experience. One popular option is Tab Suspender Pro, which helps you manage memory by automatically suspending tabs that you have not used in a while. When combined with vertical tabs, this can be a powerful combination for users who keep many tabs open simultaneously. Tab Suspender Pro adds an extra layer of organization and performance optimization to your browsing workflow, making it easier to work with large numbers of tabs without slowing down your browser.

You can find Tab Suspender Pro in the Chrome Web Store. Once installed, it works quietly in the background, suspending inactive tabs to free up memory while keeping them visible in your vertical tabs list. When you click on a suspended tab, it automatically reloads. This is especially useful if you like to keep reference materials open for later but do not need them active at all times.

If you have ever struggled with a crowded horizontal tab bar, enabling Chrome vertical tabs is definitely worth trying. The feature is free, built into Chrome, and easy to turn on. Once you see how much easier it is to find and manage your tabs, you might wonder how you ever browsed without it. Whether you are researching a topic, working on multiple projects, or just like to keep interesting articles open for later, vertical tabs can help you stay organized and productive.
