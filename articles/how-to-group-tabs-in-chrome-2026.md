# How to Group Tabs in Chrome 2026

If you have ever found yourself drowning in dozens of open tabs, struggling to find that one website you opened hours ago, you are not alone. Tab overload is one of the most common frustrations for Chrome users, and it only gets worse as we rely more on browser-based workflows for work, research, and entertainment. Fortunately, Chrome offers a powerful solution that has only gotten better in 2026: tab groups. This comprehensive guide will walk you through everything you need to know about how to group tabs in Chrome, from basic creation to advanced features like color coding, collapsing groups, saving your configurations, and sharing groups with others.

Tab groups allow you to organize your tabs into logical categories, making it easy to switch between different projects, topics, or tasks without losing your place. Whether you are managing research for a big project, keeping track of multiple shopping sites, or organizing your work and personal browsing, tab groups can transform your Chrome experience from chaotic to streamlined. In this guide, we will cover each feature in detail, providing step-by-step instructions and tips to help you become a tab grouping pro.

## Understanding Tab Groups in Chrome

Before we dive into the specifics of how to group tabs in Chrome, it is helpful to understand what tab groups are and why they matter. A tab group is essentially a folder for your tabs. Instead of having all your tabs listed in a single long row, you can organize them into colored groups that collapse and expand as needed. Each group has a name and a color, making it easy to identify at a glance.

Chrome introduced tab groups back in 2020, but the feature has evolved significantly since then. In 2026, tab groups are more powerful and intuitive than ever before. You can now save your tab groups so they persist across browser sessions, share them with others, and even use keyboard shortcuts to manage them quickly. The feature integrates seamlessly with Chrome's existing tab management tools, creating a comprehensive system for keeping your browsing organized.

One of the biggest advantages of tab groups is that they help reduce cognitive load. When you have twenty tabs open, your brain has to work harder to find what you need. By grouping related tabs together, you create a visual structure that makes it easier to locate specific pages. This is especially valuable for professionals who need to switch between different projects or clients throughout the day.

## How to Create a Tab Group in Chrome

Creating a tab group is straightforward, and there are several ways to do it. The most common method involves using right-click menus, but you can also drag and drop tabs to create groups, which many users find more intuitive.

To create a tab group using the right-click menu, start by clicking on the tab you want to include in the group. Right-click on the tab to open the context menu, then select "Add to new group" from the options. A new group will be created with that tab inside, and you will be prompted to give the group a name. Type whatever name makes sense for your organization, such as "Research," "Shopping," or "Project Alpha," and press Enter.

You can also add additional tabs to an existing group by right-clicking on a tab and selecting "Add to existing group," then choosing the group you want to add it to. This is useful when you are building your organization system incrementally throughout your browsing session.

The drag-and-drop method gives you more visual control over your groups. To use this approach, click and hold on a tab, then drag it over another tab. A visual indicator will appear showing that you can drop the tab to create a new group or add it to an existing group. This method is particularly satisfying because you can see exactly where your tab will go before you release it.

Once you have created a group, you will see a colored bar above the tabs in that group. The group name appears on this bar, and you can click the name to rename it or change its color. You can also drag additional tabs into or out of groups by dragging them onto the group's colored bar.

## Color Coding Your Tab Groups

One of the most useful features of tab groups is the ability to assign colors to different groups. Color coding helps you quickly identify groups at a glance, which is especially helpful when you have multiple groups open simultaneously. Chrome offers a range of preset colors, including gray, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, and pink.

To change the color of a tab group, click on the group name or the colored bar above the tabs. A menu will appear with color options at the top. Simply click on the color you want to use, and the group will update immediately. You can also rename the group from this menu by clicking on the current name and typing a new one.

When deciding how to color code your groups, think about creating a system that makes sense for your workflow. For example, you might use red for urgent tasks, green for personal browsing, and blue for work-related tabs. You could also use colors to represent different clients, projects, or topics. The key is to be consistent so that the colors become a meaningful visual cue rather than just decoration.

Some users find it helpful to create a color legend, either in a notes app or as a pinned tab, to remind themselves what each color represents. This is especially useful when you first start using tab groups and are developing your organizational system. Over time, the colors will become second nature, and you will find yourself navigating to the right group without even thinking about it.

## Collapsing and Expanding Tab Groups

Collapsing groups is one of the most powerful features for managing tab overload. When you collapse a group, all the tabs in that group are hidden, leaving only the group's colored bar visible. This dramatically reduces clutter and makes it easier to see your other groups and tabs. When you need to access a collapsed group's tabs, simply click on the group bar to expand it.

To collapse or expand a group, click on the small arrow icon next to the group name, or simply double-click on the group bar. You can also right-click on the group and select "Collapse group" or "Expand group" from the menu. The keyboard shortcut Ctrl plus the number key for the group can also toggle the collapse state if you have keyboard shortcuts enabled.

Collapsing groups is particularly useful when you are working on multiple projects and need to switch focus frequently. Instead of having all your tabs visible at once, you can keep only the group you are currently working on expanded, and collapse the others. This creates a cleaner, more focused workspace that helps you stay productive.

You can also set Chrome to remember which groups were collapsed when you close and reopen your browser. This ensures that your workspace stays organized exactly as you left it, so you do not have to rebuild your groups every time you start a new browsing session.

## Saving Tab Groups for Future Use

One of the most significant improvements to tab groups in recent years is the ability to save your groups so they persist across browser sessions. In the past, groups would disappear when you closed Chrome, which limited their usefulness for ongoing projects. Now, you can save your groups with a single click, ensuring they are always available when you need them.

To save a tab group, right-click on the group bar and select "Save group" from the menu. You can also click on the star icon that appears next to the group name when you hover over it. Once saved, the group will appear in a dedicated section at the top of your tab strip, separate from your unsaved groups. Saved groups persist even after you close and reopen Chrome, making them perfect for ongoing projects, recurring tasks, or topics you research regularly.

When you save a group, Chrome remembers which tabs are in the group and their order. This means you can close your browser, come back days later, and pick up exactly where you left off. Saved groups are especially valuable for workflows that span multiple days or that involve checking the same set of websites regularly.

To remove a saved group, right-click on the saved group and select "Delete saved group" or click the X icon next to the group name. This will remove the saved version but leave your current tabs intact. You can also rename saved groups by right-clicking and selecting "Rename saved group."

Managing saved groups is easy through Chrome's tab management interface. You can access all your saved groups by clicking on the "Tabs" button in the upper right corner of Chrome or by pressing Ctrl plus Shift and a number. From here, you can see all your open tabs and saved groups, making it easy to restore a saved group or move tabs between groups.

## Sharing Tab Groups with Colleagues

Chrome 2026 makes it easier than ever to share your tab groups with colleagues, friends, or family members. Whether you are collaborating on a project and want to ensure everyone has access to the same resources, or you simply want to share a collection of interesting links, sharing tab groups is a breeze.

To share a tab group, right-click on the group bar and select "Share group" from the menu. Chrome will generate a shareable link that you can send to others via email, chat, or any other communication tool. When someone clicks on the link, Chrome will open the shared group in their browser, showing all the tabs that were in the original group.

This feature is incredibly useful for teams working on shared research, content planning, or project coordination. Instead of copying and pasting links back and forth, you can simply share the entire group with everyone involved. Each person will get their own copy of the tabs, so they can reference the resources without affecting anyone else's setup.

You can also export a tab group as a list of links if you prefer not to use the built-in sharing feature. Right-click on the group and select "Copy all tab links" to copy all the URLs to your clipboard, which you can then paste into a document, email, or messaging app. This gives you more flexibility in how you share the information and allows recipients to add the links to their own tab groups in whatever way works best for them.

## Tips for Maximizing Your Tab Group Workflow

Now that you know the core features of tab groups, here are some additional tips to help you get the most out of this powerful organization tool.

First, take time to plan your group structure before you start creating groups. Think about how you typically browse and what categories make the most sense for your workflow. Some users prefer broad categories like Work, Personal, and Research, while others benefit from more specific groupings like "Client A Project," "Investment Research," or "Recipe Ideas." The right structure depends on your unique needs and browsing habits.

Second, use naming conventions consistently. If you name one group "Project Alpha," avoid naming another "alpha project" or "Alpha." Consistency makes it easier to find what you are looking for and prevents confusion when you have many groups.

Third, combine tab groups with other Chrome productivity features. For example, you can use Chrome's built-in tab search to quickly find a specific tab within a group by pressing Ctrl plus Shift and A, then typing all or part of the tab's title or URL. You can also use bookmarks in conjunction with tab groups, saving your groups as bookmarks for additional organization options.

Fourth, consider using extensions that complement tab groups. Tab Suspender Pro, for example, works alongside tab groups to automatically suspend tabs you are not actively using, which frees up memory and keeps Chrome running smoothly even when you have many groups open. This is particularly valuable if you tend to keep lots of tabs and groups active throughout the day, as it prevents your browser from slowing down.

Finally, take advantage of Chrome's sync feature to keep your tab groups across all your devices. If you use Chrome on multiple computers, phones, or tablets, your saved groups and their contents will sync automatically, allowing you to seamlessly continue your work no matter which device you are using.

## Troubleshooting Common Tab Group Issues

While tab groups are generally reliable, you may encounter occasional issues. Here are some solutions to common problems.

If a group is not saving properly, make sure you are signed into your Chrome profile, as saved groups are tied to your account. Also, check that sync is enabled in your Chrome settings. If sync is off, your saved groups may not persist across sessions or devices.

If you accidentally delete a group, do not panic. As long as the tabs from the group are still open, you can recreate the group by selecting all the tabs and adding them to a new group. Chrome does not currently have a built-in undo function for deleted groups, so prevention is key make sure you want to delete a group before confirming.

If Chrome is running slowly with many groups open, consider using Tab Suspender Pro to suspend inactive tabs. Groups with many tabs can consume significant memory, and suspending the ones you are not currently using can dramatically improve performance.
