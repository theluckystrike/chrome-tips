# How to Group Tabs in Chrome 2026

If you use Chrome regularly, you probably have dozens of tabs open at any given time. Research shows that the average Chrome user has between 10 and 30 tabs open simultaneously, and power users can easily exceed 50 or more. This tab overload can slow down your browser, make it difficult to find what you need, and decrease your overall productivity. Fortunately, Chrome offers a powerful solution: tab groups. In this guide, you'll learn everything you need to know about how to group tabs in Chrome 2026, from basic creation to advanced features like color coding, collapsing groups, saving them for later, and even sharing them with colleagues.

## Understanding Tab Groups in Chrome

Tab groups allow you to organize your tabs into logical categories, making it easier to navigate through related pages. Instead of having a chaotic mess of tabs scattered across your browser window, you can group tabs by project, topic, or any criteria that makes sense for your workflow. Each group gets its own name and color, and you can collapse or expand groups to focus on what matters at the moment.

Chrome introduced tab groups several years ago, but the feature has been significantly enhanced in recent updates. The 2026 version of Chrome includes intuitive drag-and-drop functionality, improved visual management, and new ways to save and share your tab groups. Whether you are a casual browser or a power user managing complex projects, tab groups can transform the way you work.

## Creating Your First Tab Group

Creating a tab group in Chrome 2026 is straightforward and can be done in several ways. The most common method involves using the context menu on any tab. Simply right-click on a tab you want to include in a group, then select "Add to new group" from the menu that appears. You will see a new group appear with a default name and color, and you can immediately rename it by clicking on the group name and typing your preferred label. The new group will appear in your tab strip, and you can drag other tabs into it at any time.

Another way to create groups is by dragging one tab onto another. When you drag a tab and hover it over another tab for a moment, Chrome will create a group containing both tabs. This method is particularly intuitive and allows you to quickly organize tabs as you browse. You can continue adding more tabs to the group by dragging additional tabs onto the group header. The group expands visually when you drag a tab over it, showing you exactly where the tab will be placed within the group.

If you prefer keyboard shortcuts, Chrome has you covered there as well. You can select multiple tabs using Ctrl+Click (or Cmd+Click on Mac) to highlight the tabs you want to group, then right-click and choose "Add to new group." This is especially useful when you need to group tabs that are not adjacent to each other in your tab strip. You can also use Ctrl+Shift+A (or Cmd+Shift+A on Mac) as a quick keyboard shortcut to group selected tabs without opening the context menu.

For those who want even more control, Chrome also supports creating empty groups that you can populate later. To create an empty group, simply right-click on any tab and select "Add to new group" as if you were adding that tab, but then immediately drag the tab back out of the group. The empty group will remain, ready for you to add tabs whenever you need them. This is perfect for planning your workflow ahead of time or setting up organizational structures before you start browsing.

## Mastering Color Coding for Visual Organization

One of the most powerful features of tab groups is the ability to assign colors to different groups. Color coding helps you quickly identify groups at a glance, making navigation much faster and more intuitive. Chrome 2026 offers a palette of eight distinct colors: grey, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, and pink. Each color can be used to represent different project types, priorities, or any system that works for you. The colors are deliberately chosen to be visually distinct and easily recognizable, even when you have multiple groups open.

To change the color of a group, simply right-click on the group header and hover over "Color." You will see the color palette appear, allowing you to choose the color that best fits your organization system. You might use red for urgent tasks, green for completed projects, blue for research, or whatever system makes sense to you. The key is consistency—once you establish a color system, it becomes second nature to navigate your tab groups. The colors extend across the entire group header, making it impossible to miss even when you have dozens of tabs open.

Chrome also remembers the colors you assign to specific group names, so if you frequently create groups with the same name, they will automatically adopt the color you previously assigned. This feature saves time and ensures visual consistency across your browser. You can also customize the default colors for new groups by adjusting Chrome's settings, though this option is somewhat buried in the browser's advanced preferences.

When choosing colors for your groups, consider the psychological aspects of color coding. Red naturally draws attention and works well for time-sensitive or high-priority items. Green suggests completion or ongoing work that is on track. Blue often represents research or learning materials. Yellow and orange can indicate items that need attention soon but are not yet critical. Using colors consistently across all your groups creates a visual language that speeds up your browsing significantly.

## Collapsing and Expanding Groups for Focus

As your collection of tab groups grows, you may find that the group headers themselves take up too much space in your tab strip. Chrome 2026 addresses this with the ability to collapse groups. When a group is collapsed, only the group header remains visible, showing the group name and color. All the tabs within that group are hidden from view, freeing up space for other groups or active tabs. This creates a much cleaner interface while still keeping all your organized tabs accessible.

To collapse or expand a group, simply click on the small arrow icon next to the group name in the header. You can also right-click on the group header and select "Collapse group" or "Expand group" from the context menu. This feature is incredibly valuable when you are working on multiple projects and need to focus on one while keeping others accessible but out of the way. The transition between collapsed and expanded states is animated smoothly, so you always know what is happening in your browser.

Collapsed groups still show the number of tabs they contain, so you always know how many pages are waiting in each group. This gives you the benefits of organization without the visual clutter of having every single tab visible at all times. The number appears as a small badge on the group header, and it updates automatically as you add or remove tabs from the group. This feature is particularly useful when you are juggling multiple research projects or client accounts and need to keep track of how much content you have saved in each location.

Chrome 2026 also introduces automatic collapsing based on inactivity. You can configure Chrome to automatically collapse groups that have not been used for a specified period, helping you maintain a clean workspace without manual effort. This feature works particularly well when combined with tab suspension, as both features contribute to a streamlined browsing experience that prioritizes your current work while keeping everything else organized and accessible.

## Saving Groups for Future Use

One of the most powerful additions to Chrome 2026 is the ability to save tab groups for later use. This feature is a game-changer for anyone who works on recurring projects or needs to switch between different contexts throughout their day. Instead of leaving all your tabs open or manually bookmarking each one, you can save an entire group and restore it whenever you need it. This is particularly valuable for people who work on multiple projects simultaneously or who need to step away from their computer frequently.

To save a group, right-click on the group header and select "Save group." Chrome will create a bookmark folder containing all the tabs in that group, stored in a special "Tab Groups" section in your bookmarks. You can name the saved group anything you like, and it will persist even after you close Chrome. The saved groups are stored locally on your computer, so they remain available whenever you use Chrome on that device. This means you can close all your tabs at the end of the day and come back the next morning to pick up exactly where you left off.

When you need to restore a saved group, simply navigate to your bookmarks and locate the group you want to open. You can also right-click on any tab and select "Open saved group" to see your available saved groups. Chrome will open all the tabs from the saved group in new tabs, maintaining their original order. This makes it incredibly easy to pick up exactly where you left off on any project. The tabs will open in the same sequence they were in when you saved the group, preserving your workflow.

For even more convenience, you can install extensions that enhance Chrome's native tab group saving capabilities. Some extensions offer additional features like automatic saving, cloud synchronization across devices, and more sophisticated organization tools. However, the built-in saving feature in Chrome 2026 is robust enough for most users' needs. The native feature integrates seamlessly with Chrome's sync capabilities, so if you sign in to Chrome with your Google account, your saved groups will be available across all your devices.

One particularly useful feature of saved groups is the ability to create templates. You can save a group with placeholder tabs or generic URLs that you frequently use together, then duplicate and customize the template for different purposes. This is excellent for recurring workflows like daily standup meetings, weekly report preparation, or any repetitive process that involves the same set of resources.

## Sharing Tab Groups with Others

Collaboration is easier than ever with Chrome 2026's tab group sharing features. Whether you are working on a team project or want to share a collection of resources with a friend, you can export your tab groups and share them in several ways. This feature has become increasingly important as remote work and digital collaboration have become the norm. Being able to share a curated collection of resources with colleagues or friends can save everyone time and ensure everyone has access to the same information.

The simplest method is to use the saved group feature mentioned above. Since saved groups are stored as bookmarks, you can export them as an HTML file that others can import into their own browsers. To do this, go to your bookmarks manager, locate the folder containing your saved group, and use the export function. The resulting HTML file can be shared via email, messaging apps, or cloud storage. Recipients can then import the file to get all the tabs in their own Chrome browser. This method preserves the order of tabs and any notes or metadata you have added.

For more advanced sharing, consider using dedicated extensions that specialize in tab sharing. These tools often allow you to share groups via a link that recipients can open with a single click, making it effortless to collaborate on research, plan trips, or curate resources together. Some extensions even allow real-time collaboration, where multiple people can add or remove tabs from a shared group. This is particularly useful for team projects where different people are researching different aspects of a topic.

When sharing tab groups, it is worth considering which tabs might not be relevant to others. You can create a filtered version of your group that includes only the tabs you want to share, excluding personal tabs or items that are not relevant to the recipient. This thoughtful approach to sharing ensures that you are providing genuine value without overwhelming others with unnecessary content.

## Combining Tab Groups with Tab Suspender Pro

While tab groups help you organize your tabs, managing too many open tabs can still impact your browser's performance. This is where **Tab Suspender Pro** comes in. This extension works seamlessly with Chrome's tab groups to automatically suspend tabs that you are not actively using. Suspended tabs consume minimal memory, keeping your browser running smoothly even with hundreds of tabs organized into groups.

**Tab Suspender Pro** intelligently detects which tabs are idle and puts them to sleep, freeing up system resources for the tabs you are currently using. When you return to a suspended tab, it automatically reloads in the background. This combination of tab grouping and intelligent tab suspension gives you the best of both worlds: organized tabs that are easy to navigate, plus optimal performance regardless of how many tabs you have open.

Using **Tab Suspender Pro** alongside Chrome's native tab groups creates a powerful productivity system. You can group your tabs by project, color-code them for quick identification, collapse groups to reduce visual clutter, and let the extension handle resource management automatically. This approach is particularly valuable for researchers, developers, writers, and anyone who works with large amounts of information across multiple projects.

## Tips for Effective Tab Group Management

Now that you understand the core features, here are some best practices to get the most out of tab groups in Chrome 2026. First, establish a consistent naming convention for your groups. Use clear, descriptive names that immediately convey the group's purpose. Avoid generic names like "Group 1" or "Work" when you can be more specific like "Client Project Q1" or "Research: AI Tools."

Second, develop a color system and stick to it. Consider assigning colors based on project type, priority level, or timeframe. For example, you might use red for urgent items, yellow for ongoing projects, and green for completed work. Consistency makes it easier to find what you need quickly.

Third, regularly review and clean up your groups. At the end of each week or project cycle, go through your groups and close tabs you no longer need. Move completed projects to your saved groups and start fresh. This prevents tab clutter from accumulating over time.

Fourth, take advantage of nested groups if you need additional organization. You can create groups within groups to handle complex projects with multiple subcomponents. This works especially well for large research projects or ongoing work with distinct phases.

Finally, remember to use keyboard shortcuts to speed up your workflow. The combination of keyboard navigation and tab groups can dramatically reduce the time you spend managing your browser.

Combined with tools like **Tab Suspender Pro** for performance management, you have everything you need to take control of your browsing experience. Start implementing these strategies today, and you'll wonder how you ever managed without tab groups.
