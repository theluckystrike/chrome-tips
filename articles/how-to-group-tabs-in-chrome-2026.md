# How to Group Tabs in Chrome 2026

If you have ever found yourself drowning in dozens of open tabs, you are not alone. Modern web browsing often means keeping dozens of websites open simultaneously for work, research, shopping, and entertainment. Chrome's tab grouping feature, significantly enhanced in recent years, offers an elegant solution to organize your browsing chaos. This comprehensive guide walks you through everything you need to know about how to group tabs in Chrome in 2026.

## Understanding Chrome Tab Groups

Chrome tab groups allow you to organize related tabs under a single collapsible header. Rather than having a cluttered tab bar with dozens of tiny tab previews, you can group tabs by project, topic, or any criteria that makes sense for your workflow. Each group appears as a colored bar with a label, making it easy to identify and manage your open pages at a glance.

Tab groups have evolved considerably since their introduction. In 2026, Chrome offers a robust set of features including color customization, nested groups, automatic grouping suggestions, and seamless synchronization across your devices. Whether you are managing a complex research project or simply trying to keep your personal and work browsing separate, tab groups provide the organization tools you need.

The beauty of tab groups lies in their flexibility. You can create groups on the fly, drag tabs between groups, color-code groups for visual distinction, and even save groups for future sessions. This level of control transforms Chrome from a simple browser into a powerful productivity platform.

## Creating Your First Tab Group

Creating a tab group in Chrome is straightforward and can be done in several ways depending on your preference and workflow.

### Method One: Drag and Drop

The most intuitive way to create a tab group is through drag and drop. First, click on a tab you want to include in a group. Hold your mouse button down and drag the tab slightly to the right, below the existing tab bar area. As you drag, you will see a visual indicator showing where the tab will land. Continue dragging until you see a horizontal line appear across the tab bar, then release the mouse button. This action automatically creates a new group containing that tab.

To add more tabs to your new group, simply drag additional tabs onto the group bar. Chrome will highlight the group when you hover over it with a tab, indicating that releasing the mouse button will add the tab to that group. You can also drag tabs out of a group by pulling them to the left, above the group bar, which removes them from the group.

### Method Two: Right-Click Context Menu

Another way to create tab groups is through the context menu. Right-click on any tab and select "Add to new group" from the menu that appears. You can also select multiple tabs first by holding down the Ctrl or Command key while clicking on each tab you want to include, then right-click and choose "Add to new group." This method is particularly useful when you already have several tabs open that you want to organize together.

### Method Three: Using the Tab Search Feature

Chrome's tab search feature, accessible by clicking the arrow next to your profile picture or pressing Ctrl or Command plus Shift plus A, provides another way to manage groups. While primarily designed for finding open tabs across all your windows, it also displays group information and allows you to move tabs between groups quickly.

## Color Coding Your Tab Groups

Color coding transforms your tab groups from mere organizational containers into visual navigation aids. Chrome offers eight distinct colors: grey, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, and pink. Each color serves as a visual anchor, helping you quickly locate specific groups among dozens of open tabs.

To change a group's color, right-click on the group header and hover over "Select color" from the menu. A color palette appears, showing all available options. Click your desired color, and the group header immediately updates to reflect your choice. You can change colors as often as you like, making it easy to update your organization system as your needs evolve.

Effective color coding strategies can significantly improve your productivity. Consider assigning specific colors to different project types or work contexts. For example, you might use blue for work-related groups, green for personal research, purple for shopping, and red for urgent tasks. This consistent color scheme becomes intuitive over time, allowing you to locate groups instantly without reading their labels.

The color you choose also appears on individual tabs within the group, creating a cohesive visual identity. When you minimize or collapse a group, the colored bar remains visible, ensuring you never lose track of where you have organized your tabs. This visual consistency extends across all your Chrome windows, maintaining organization regardless of which window you are working in.

## Collapsing and Expanding Tab Groups

One of the most powerful features of tab groups is the ability to collapse and expand them. A collapsed group shows only its header bar, displaying the group name and the number of tabs it contains. This compact view dramatically reduces visual clutter while keeping all your tabs accessible.

To collapse a group, click the downward-pointing arrow on the group header, or simply double-click the header itself. The tabs within the group disappear from view, replaced by a slim bar showing the group name and tab count. Click the arrow again or double-click to expand the group and reveal all its tabs.

Collapsed groups are particularly valuable when you are working on multiple projects simultaneously. You can keep all your research tabs organized in one group, all your email and communication tabs in another, and all your reference materials in a third. When you need to focus on one project, collapse the other groups to clear your view. When you need to switch context, expand the relevant group and pick up exactly where you left off.

The collapse feature also helps with performance. While Chrome still keeps all tabs active in the background, collapsing groups can make the interface feel more responsive by reducing the number of visible elements. For an even more aggressive approach to managing tab resources, consider using Tab Suspender Pro, which automatically suspends inactive tabs to free up memory while maintaining the same convenient group organization.

## Saving Tab Groups for Future Use

By default, when you close Chrome, your tab groups disappear along with your browsing session. However, Chrome offers several ways to save and restore groups for future use, ensuring your carefully organized tabs are available whenever you need them.

### Using Bookmarks

The simplest way to save a tab group is through bookmarks. Right-click on the group header and select "Bookmark group" from the menu. A dialog appears where you can choose the folder where you want to save the bookmark, name the group, and decide whether to include the group's folder structure. Once bookmarked, you can restore the entire group with a single click anytime you open Chrome.

This method works particularly well for groups you use regularly, such as your morning routine tabs (email, calendar, news) or your project-specific research collections. You can even create desktop shortcuts to these bookmarked groups for even faster access.

### Using Chrome's Session Management

Chrome automatically saves your open tabs and groups when you exit the browser, restoring them when you next open Chrome. This default behavior can be customized in Chrome Settings under "On startup." You can choose to restore all tabs, open the New Tab page, or open a specific set of pages.

For more advanced session management, consider using Chrome's built-in tab groups sync feature. When you sign into Chrome with your Google account, your tab groups synchronize across all your devices. Create a group on your work computer, and it appears on your home computer or mobile device. This cross-device synchronization ensures your organization system travels with you wherever you browse.

### Creating Permanent Group Collections

For long-term organization, you might want to create what essentially amounts to permanent collections. Instead of saving individual groups as bookmarks, create a dedicated bookmark folder for each major area of your browsing. Add relevant tabs to these folders as you browse, and open the entire folder when you start a work session. This approach provides maximum flexibility while maintaining consistent organization over time.

## Sharing Tab Groups with Others

Collaboration is easier when you can share your organized tab groups with colleagues, teammates, or friends. Chrome provides several methods for sharing tabs and groups, each suited to different use cases.

### Sharing via Bookmark Export

The most straightforward way to share a tab group is by exporting it as a bookmark file. Select all tabs in the group by right-clicking and choosing "Select all in group," then right-click again and choose "Bookmark these tabs." This creates a bookmark folder containing all the group's tabs, which you can then export and share.

To export bookmarks, open the Bookmark Manager (Ctrl or Command plus Shift plus O), locate your exported folder, and use the menu to export all bookmarks to an HTML file. Send this file to anyone who needs access to your group. They can import it into their own Chrome browser and immediately access all the same tabs.

### Sharing via Tab Sharing Extensions

Several Chrome extensions provide more sophisticated sharing features. These extensions allow you to create shareable links for entire tab groups, making it easy to collaborate on research projects or share resource collections with others. Some popular options include Tabler, which provides enhanced tab group management including sharing capabilities, and various clipboard and note-taking extensions that integrate with tab management.

When choosing a sharing extension, look for ones that offer real-time synchronization, password protection for sensitive shared groups, and easy import and export options. Many of these extensions integrate seamlessly with Chrome's native tab group features, enhancing rather than replacing them.

### Collaborative Workflows

For ongoing collaboration, consider establishing shared naming conventions and color schemes with your team. When everyone uses the same organizational system, sharing becomes more intuitive. Create a standard set of colored groups for common project types, and document your group's organization strategy so new team members can quickly adopt your system.

Tab groups also work well for handoff scenarios. When you need to transfer work to a colleague, simply share the relevant tab group rather than trying to explain which websites and resources you were using. This ensures nothing gets lost in translation and your colleague has immediate access to all the same context.

## Advanced Tab Group Tips and Tricks

Mastering tab groups involves more than just the basics. These advanced tips will help you get the most out of Chrome's grouping capabilities.

### Keyboard Shortcuts

Chrome offers several keyboard shortcuts for working with tab groups. Press Ctrl or Command plus Shift plus E to move the current tab to a new group. Use the arrow keys while holding Ctrl or Command to move tabs between groups. These shortcuts become invaluable as you develop muscle memory for common organization tasks.

### Naming Groups Effectively

A well-named group is easier to find and understand at a glance. Use descriptive, specific names that clearly indicate the group's purpose. Instead of "Project," try "Q1 Marketing Campaign" or "Client: Acme Corp Research." Avoid overly long names that clutter the interface, but err on the side of clarity.

### Nested Groups

Chrome supports creating groups within groups, allowing for hierarchical organization. To create a nested group, drag one group onto another. This is useful for complex projects with multiple sub-projects or when you want to separate different aspects of your work within a larger context.

### Group Locking

For groups you access frequently and want to prevent accidentally modifying, right-click the group header and select "Lock group." Locked groups cannot have tabs added, removed, or reordered without first unlocking them. This feature prevents accidental changes to your most important organizational structures.

Tab groups represent one of Chrome's most powerful productivity features, and the enhancements made through 2026 have made them more capable than ever. By mastering tab group creation, color coding, collapsing, saving, and sharing, you can transform your browsing experience from chaotic to organized.

Start with the basics: create your first group, experiment with colors, and practice collapsing and expanding. As these skills become automatic, move on to advanced techniques like bookmarking groups for future use and sharing them with colleagues. Remember that the best tab group system is one you actually use. Start simple, build habits gradually, and let your organization system evolve as your needs change.
