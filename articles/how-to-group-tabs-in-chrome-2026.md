# How to Group Tabs in Chrome 2026

If you frequently find yourself juggling dozens of open tabs in Google Chrome, you're not alone. The average Chrome user has around 70 tabs open at any given time, and managing this overwhelming number of open pages can quickly become a productivity nightmare. Fortunately, Chrome's built-in tab grouping feature offers an elegant solution to this modern problem. In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through everything you need to know about how to group tabs in Chrome 2026, from basic creation to advanced techniques that will transform your browsing experience.

Tab groups have become an essential tool for anyone who wants to stay organized while browsing the web. Whether you're a professional managing multiple projects, a student researching different topics, or simply someone who likes to keep their browsing organized, mastering tab groups will significantly improve your workflow. Let's dive into the details and explore all the features that make tab groups one of Chrome's most powerful organizational tools.

## Understanding Tab Groups in Chrome

Tab groups in Chrome allow you to organize your open tabs into labeled, color-coded collections that make it easy to find what you need quickly. Instead of scrolling through an endless list of tabs or using keyboard shortcuts to cycle through them, you can simply click on a group name and see all related tabs at once. This feature has evolved significantly over the years, and the 2026 version offers more customization and flexibility than ever before.

The beauty of tab groups lies in their simplicity and effectiveness. When you create a group, all the tabs within that group are visually connected by a colored bar and a label that you choose. This visual organization makes it immediately apparent which tabs belong together, reducing the cognitive load of managing multiple open pages. You can create groups for different projects, research topics, social media sites, or any other categorization that makes sense for your workflow.

One of the most significant advantages of tab groups is that they persist across browser sessions. When you close Chrome and reopen it later, your tab groups will still be there, exactly as you left them. This persistence means you can set up your ideal browsing environment once and then rely on it being there whenever you need it. Of course, this also means you should be mindful about which tabs you're keeping open, as having too many groups with too many tabs can still lead to clutter and reduced performance.

## Creating Your First Tab Group

Creating a tab group in Chrome 2026 is straightforward and can be done in several ways depending on your preference. The most common method involves right-clicking on a tab and selecting "Add to new group" from the context menu. This will create a new group with that tab already included, and you can then name the group and choose a color for it. This method is perfect for when you have a few tabs open and want to start organizing them from scratch.

Alternatively, you can create a group by selecting multiple tabs first. To select multiple tabs, click on one tab, then hold down the Shift key and click on another tab to select a range, or hold down the Command key (on Mac) or Control key (on Windows) to select individual tabs. Once you've selected the tabs you want to group together, right-click on any of the selected tabs and choose "Add to new group." This method is much faster when you already have several tabs open that you know should be grouped together.

Another approach is to create an empty group first and then add tabs to it as needed. To create an empty group, right-click on any tab and select "Add to new group," then choose "New group" from the submenu. This creates a group without any tabs that you can populate over time. This method works well if you want to plan your organization structure ahead of time or if you prefer to add tabs to groups as you encounter them throughout your browsing session.

Once you've created a group, you can easily rename it by clicking on the group name and typing a new one. You can also change the group's color by clicking on the colored dot next to the group name and selecting from the available options. Taking a moment to choose descriptive names and appropriate colors will make your groups easier to identify at a glance, which is especially helpful when you have multiple groups open simultaneously.

## Color Coding Your Tab Groups

Color coding is one of the most powerful features of Chrome's tab group system. By assigning different colors to different groups, you can quickly identify the type or category of content in each group without even reading the group name. This visual recognition system works similarly to color-coded file folders in a physical filing cabinet, allowing your brain to process information more efficiently.

Chrome offers eight distinct colors for you to choose from: grey, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, pink, and purple. Each color can convey different meanings depending on your personal organization system. For example, you might use red for urgent or time-sensitive projects, green for ongoing work, blue for reference materials, and purple for personal browsing. There's no right or wrong way to use colors—the key is to be consistent so that your brain learns to associate each color with its meaning.

When choosing colors for your groups, consider the psychological associations that different colors carry. Red tends to draw attention and might be best reserved for groups that contain important or time-sensitive information. Blue is often associated with productivity and work-related tasks. Green suggests growth or ongoing projects, making it suitable for work in progress. Yellow and orange can indicate items that need attention or review. Using these associations thoughtfully can help you navigate your tabs more intuitively.

The process of changing a group's color is simple. Click on the colored dot next to the group name in the tab bar, and a palette of available colors will appear. Simply click on your desired color, and the change will apply immediately. You can also right-click on the group name and select "Choose color" from the context menu to access the same color selection interface. Experiment with different color schemes until you find one that feels intuitive and natural to you.

## Managing Collapsed and Expanded Groups

One of the most useful features of tab groups is the ability to collapse and expand them. When a group is collapsed, only the group name and color are visible, along with a small indicator showing how many of tabs are in that group. This dramatically reduces visual clutter and makes it much easier to see all your groups at once. When you need to access the tabs within a collapsed group, simply click on the group name to expand it and reveal all the tabs inside.

Collapsing groups is particularly helpful when you're working on multiple projects simultaneously and need to switch between different contexts quickly. Instead of scrolling through dozens of individual tabs, you can see your groups at a glance and click on the one you need. This workflow is much more efficient than having all tabs visible at once, especially when you're working with a large number of open tabs.

You can collapse or expand a group by clicking on the small arrow or chevron icon next to the group name. Alternatively, you can right-click on the group name and select "Collapse group" or "Expand group" from the context menu. Chrome also remembers your collapse/expand state, so if you collapse a group before closing Chrome, it will still be collapsed when you next open the browser.

For power users, there are keyboard shortcuts that can speed up this process even further. You can use keyboard shortcuts to quickly collapse or expand all groups at once, which is handy when you need to quickly clear your view or see everything at a glance. These shortcuts can be particularly useful in combination with other Chrome keyboard shortcuts for tab management.

The collapsed state also provides a visual indicator of how many tabs are in each group, shown as a number next to the group name. This count helps you understand the scope of each group without having to expand it, which can be helpful for prioritizing which groups to focus on. If you find that a particular group has too many tabs, it might be a sign that you should consider breaking it into smaller, more focused sub-groups.

## Saving and Preserving Your Tab Groups

While Chrome automatically remembers your tab groups when you close and reopen the browser, there are additional ways to save and manage your groups for the long term. One of the most effective approaches is to bookmark your tab groups, which allows you to restore them at any time even if something goes wrong with your browser settings or you want to share them with another device.

To bookmark an entire tab group, right-click on the group name and select "Bookmark group" from the context menu. This will open a dialog where you can choose where to save the bookmark in your bookmark folder hierarchy. You might want to create a dedicated folder for all your tab group bookmarks, which makes it easy to find and restore them later. Give the bookmark a descriptive name that will help you remember what tabs are in that group.

When you need to restore a bookmarked group, simply navigate to your bookmarks and click on the group bookmark. Chrome will open all the tabs in that group in new windows or add them to your current window, depending on your preference. This feature is incredibly valuable for recurring workflows, such as starting your workday with a specific set of research tabs or project resources.

Chrome's sync functionality also helps preserve your tab groups across devices. If you're signed in to Chrome with your Google account, your tab groups will sync automatically to other devices where you're also signed in. This means you can start working on your desktop computer and continue seamlessly on your laptop or mobile device. However, it's worth noting that sync behavior can sometimes be inconsistent, so it's still a good idea to maintain backups of important groups as bookmarks.

For users who need even more robust preservation, third-party extensions can provide additional features for saving, exporting, and restoring tab groups. Some of these extensions offer advanced features like scheduled backups, group templates, and the ability to export groups to files that can be shared with others. If you rely heavily on tab groups for your work, exploring these extension options might be worthwhile.

## Enhancing Tab Groups with Tab Suspender Pro

While Chrome's built-in tab groups are powerful, combining them with other productivity extensions can take your browsing organization to the next level. One particularly useful companion to tab groups is Tab Suspender Pro, an extension that automatically suspends tabs you're not actively using to save memory and improve browser performance. This combination allows you to keep many tabs organized in groups without worrying about the performance impact of having numerous inactive tabs open.

Tab Suspender Pro works by detecting when a tab hasn't been used for a specified period and "sleeping" it, which releases the memory it was consuming. When you click on the sleeping tab, it automatically reloads. This means you can keep all your organized groups visible and ready to use without experiencing the slowdowns that typically come with having too many tabs open. The extension respects your tab groups and can be configured to suspend tabs based on various criteria.

The integration between tab groups and Tab Suspender Pro is seamless. When you have a group with many tabs, you can set Tab Suspender Pro to automatically suspend all tabs in that group after a period of inactivity, or you can manually trigger suspension for specific groups. This gives you the best of both worlds: organized, color-coded groups for easy navigation, and automatic memory management to keep your browser running smoothly.

To get the most out of this combination, consider which groups contain tabs you access frequently versus those you only need occasionally. For groups with reference materials or resources you check periodically, Tab Suspender Pro can automatically manage them in the background. For groups with tabs you use throughout the day, you might want to adjust the suspension settings to keep those tabs active. This level of customization allows you to optimize your browsing experience for both organization and performance.

## Sharing Tab Groups with Colleagues

Collaboration is a key part of modern work, and Chrome's tab groups can facilitate sharing information with colleagues efficiently. While there's no built-in "share group" feature in Chrome, you can easily share all the URLs in a tab group with others through a few different methods. This makes it simple to share research resources, project references, or any collection of related web pages with team members.

The simplest way to share a tab group is to copy all the URLs and paste them into an email, chat message, or document. To do this, right-click on the group name and select "Copy all URLs" from the context menu. This will copy all the URLs in the group to your clipboard, and you can then paste them wherever you need to share them. The recipient can click on these links to open the pages in their own browser, though they'll need to create their own tab groups to organize them.

For a more polished sharing experience, you can use bookmark export features or third-party extensions designed specifically for sharing tab collections. Some extensions allow you to export a group as a formatted list or even generate a shareable link that others can use to import the group directly into their browser. These tools can be particularly valuable for teams that regularly share web resources.

Another approach is to use Chrome's built-in sharing features in combination with your operating system's capabilities. You can select all tabs in a group, copy them, and then paste them into a shared document or project management tool. Some teams maintain living documents with regularly updated lists of important resources, and this workflow integrates well with such systems.

For permanent team resources, consider setting up a shared bookmark folder that everyone on the team can access. This way, whenever someone updates the shared resources, everyone else automatically sees the changes. This approach works particularly well for ongoing projects where the team frequently needs access to the same set of web resources.

## Advanced Tab Group Tips and Tricks

Now that you understand the fundamentals of tab groups, let's explore some advanced tips and tricks that can help you get even more out of this feature. One powerful technique is to use nested groups, which allows you to create hierarchies within your tab organization. While Chrome doesn't officially support nested groups, you can simulate this effect by using consistent naming conventions, such as prefixing group names with project codes or categories.

Another advanced technique involves using the tab search feature in combination with groups. Chrome's tab search (accessible by pressing Command+Shift+A on Mac or Control+Shift+A on Windows) allows you to quickly find any open tab across all your groups. This is incredibly useful when you have many groups and even more tabs, as it provides a fast way to locate specific content without manually expanding groups.

You can also customize how new tabs are added to groups. By default, when you open a new tab, it appears outside of any group. However, you can use Chrome's tab group settings or third-party extensions to automatically add new tabs to specific groups based on rules you define. For example, you might automatically add any new tab that contains the word "shopping" to a Shopping group, or route all YouTube links to a Media group.

For users who work with many windows simultaneously, understanding how tab groups interact with multiple windows is important. Each Chrome window maintains its own set of tab groups, which means you can have different group configurations in different windows. This can be useful for separating different projects or contexts, though it requires some mental tracking to remember which groups are in which windows.

Finally, consider establishing a regular maintenance routine for your tab groups. Just like physical clutter can accumulate in your home, digital clutter can build up in your browser over time. Set aside a few minutes each week to review your groups, close tabs you no longer need, and reorganize any groups that have become disorganized. This maintenance will keep your browsing environment clean and productive in the long run.
